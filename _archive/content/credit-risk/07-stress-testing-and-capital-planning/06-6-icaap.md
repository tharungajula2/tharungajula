# §6 · ICAAP

## 6.1 The three pillars, and what Pillar 2 is actually for

| Pillar | Content |
|:--|:--|
| **Pillar 1** | Minimum capital for credit, market and operational risk — formula-driven, Document 06 §7 |
| **Pillar 2** | Supervisory review, and the bank's own **Internal Capital Adequacy Assessment Process** |
| **Pillar 3** | Market discipline through disclosure |

📘 **Pillar 1 covers three risks with prescribed formulas. Pillar 2 covers everything else** — and "everything else" is where most of the risks that have actually killed banks live.

## 6.2 What ICAAP must address beyond Pillar 1

- **Concentration risk** — single borrower, group, sector, geography. Pillar 1's standardised approach is blind to it: a portfolio of one hundred loans to the same sector and one hundred diversified loans carry identical RWA. This is the largest single Pillar 1 gap.
- **Interest rate risk in the banking book (IRRBB)** — economic value and earnings sensitivity to rate movements.
- **Liquidity and funding risk**, interacting with capital.
- **Business and strategic risk** — the risk that the business model stops working.
- **Reputational risk** — and Document 02 §10.4's conduct rules are the live channel for this in retail lending.
- **Model risk** — now with an explicit framework pending, per Document 04's opening.
- **Residual credit risk** — where credit risk mitigation does not perform as assumed. Document 06 §7.5: an unenforceable security has no capital value.

## 6.3 The structure of an ICAAP document

Risk identification and materiality assessment → the internal capital requirement for each material risk → the capital plan across a three-to-five-year horizon → stress testing including reverse stress testing → a contingency capital plan → governance, board approval and the **use test**.

🔴 **The use test is the part that matters and the part most often failed.** The question a supervisor actually asks is not "is your ICAAP well written?" It is: **"show me a business decision that changed because of it."** An ICAAP produced by a small team, approved by the board and filed is a compliance artefact. An ICAAP whose capital allocation shapes next year's product plan is a management tool. **The distinction is visible immediately to anyone who asks the right question, and it is the right question to ask in an interview about the function you would be joining.**
