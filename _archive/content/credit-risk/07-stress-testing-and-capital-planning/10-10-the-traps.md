# §10 · THE TRAPS

1. **Conflating ECL with stress loss.** ECL weights the downside by its probability; a stress test assumes it happens. §1.1.
2. **Quoting Basel minimums for India.** India is 9% CRAR, not 8%. §2.1.
3. **Treating a buffer as a minimum.** It is meant to be used; breaching it restricts distributions, it does not breach the requirement. §2.2.
4. **Comparing the FSR's 8.1% sensitivity with its 4.1% adverse scenario.** Different exercises. §3.2.
5. **Reading the FSR bank results and ignoring the NBFC results**, which are worse and matter more for household credit. §3.2.
6. **Presenting a stress test without naming the weakest link** — the macro-to-parameter satellite models. §4.1.
7. **Calibrating scenario severity only on history.** Severe stresses are mostly things that have not happened. §4.2.
8. **Reporting the capital ratio and not the P&L.** Boards feel earnings first. §4.3.
9. **Treating reverse stress testing as a formality.** §5.2.
10. **Assuming Pillar 1 covers concentration risk.** It does not — identical RWA for a concentrated and a diversified book. §6.2.
11. **Judging an ICAAP by its document rather than by the use test.** §6.3.
12. **Comparing products on margin rather than RAROC.** The home loan wins at a third of the spread. §7.2.
13. **Computing RAROC off floored provisions**, which understates true return by about a third where the floor binds. §7.2.
14. **Modelling post-2027 stress through NPA formation** rather than Stage 2 migration. §8.1.
15. **Criticising the prudential floors without noting they are counter-cyclical.** §8.2.
