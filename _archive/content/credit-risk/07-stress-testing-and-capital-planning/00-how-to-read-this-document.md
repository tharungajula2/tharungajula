*Projecting the balance sheet forward under conditions that have not happened. The capital stack, the RBI's macro stress test, a bank's own scenario design, reverse stress testing, ICAAP, and the capital planning cycle that turns everything in Documents 03 to 06 into a forward view.*

## HOW TO READ THIS DOCUMENT

Documents 03 to 06 measured a portfolio and valued it at a point in time. This one asks the question a board actually asks: **what happens to us if things go badly, and do we have enough capital to survive it?**

It is also the document that connects your work to the room where decisions get made. Vintage curves and WoE tables do not go to a board. **Stress test results and capital plans do.** If you want to understand what the senior people in a risk function spend their time on, it is largely this.

Read §2 carefully and be able to reproduce the capital stack from memory. It is the most reliably asked factual content in this document, and getting India's numbers right — which differ from Basel's — is a small, cheap credibility win.

> **The one sentence that organises this document**
>
> ECL asks *what do we expect to lose across all scenarios?* A stress test asks *what happens if the bad scenario simply occurs?* — and the difference between a probability-weighted answer and an unweighted one is the entire reason capital exists on top of provisions.

## UPDATES SINCE DOCUMENT 06

Three items, all directly load-bearing here.

### ✅ The capital adequacy framework has been consolidated 

The **RBI (Commercial Banks – Prudential Norms on Capital Adequacy) Directions, 2025**, issued November 2025 and updated March 2026, consolidated the capital adequacy framework for commercial banks into a single instrument — folding the **D-SIB surcharge, the capital conservation buffer and the countercyclical capital buffer** into one entity-specific direction rather than leaving them scattered across overlapping circulars.

📘 This sits alongside, not inside, the **Capital Charge for Credit Risk – Standardised Approach Directions, 2026** of Document 06 §7. **The 2025 Directions set how much capital you must hold; the 2026 Directions set how RWA is computed.** Denominator and numerator, two instruments.

### ✅ The CCyB remains un-activated 

On **18 May 2026** the RBI announced it had again decided **not to activate** the countercyclical capital buffer, stating that prevailing systemic risk conditions did not warrant it. Credit growth was assessed as stable with no signs of excessive lending.

**India has never activated the CCyB** since the framework was put in place in February 2015. The main indicator is the **credit-to-GDP gap**, used with supplementary indicators including the credit-to-deposit ratio, the Industrial Outlook Assessment Survey, interest coverage ratios and asset quality. Decisions are normally pre-announced.

🔴 **Worth having ready, because it is a good question to be asked.** Document 01 showed retail credit growing 16.6% and gold at 50.4%. Why no CCyB? Because the trigger is the **credit-to-GDP gap** — credit growth relative to the trend of the economy as a whole — not the growth rate of any one segment. Rapid growth in a segment is addressed with **sectoral** tools: the November 2023 risk-weight action, the gold LTV directions, the digital lending rules. **The CCyB is a whole-system instrument; India has consistently preferred targeted sectoral instruments instead**, and that is a deliberate and defensible policy choice rather than an oversight.
