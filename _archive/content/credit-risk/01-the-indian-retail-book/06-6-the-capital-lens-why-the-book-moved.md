# §6 · THE CAPITAL LENS — WHY THE BOOK MOVED

The most important causal mechanism in this document. Almost every compositional shift described in §1 and §2 traces back to capital, not to demand.

## 6.1 What a risk weight does

📘 **Risk weight.** Under the standardised approach for credit risk, risk-weighted assets equal exposure times risk weight. The regulatory capital requirement is a percentage of RWA. Raise the risk weight and you raise the capital a lender must hold against the same rupee of the same loan — which raises the return that loan must earn to be worth writing.

🧮 **Worked.** ₹4,000 crore of personal loans at a 100% risk weight produces ₹4,000 crore of RWA; at a 9% capital requirement, ₹360 crore of capital. Move the risk weight to 125% and RWA becomes ₹5,000 crore and the capital requirement ₹450 crore. Same loans, same borrowers, same expected losses — ₹90 crore more capital tied up. Since capital has a required return, the loan must now be priced higher or not written. **The regulator did not ban anything. It made the marginal account uneconomic, which is more effective and more precise.**

## 6.2 The November 2023 tightening 

| Exposure | Before | After |
|:--|--:|--:|
| Consumer credit — banks | 100% | **125%** |
| Consumer credit — NBFCs | 100% | **125%** |
| Credit card receivables — banks | 125% | **150%** |
| Credit card receivables — NBFCs | 100% | **125%** |
| Bank exposure to NBFCs (where externally-rated weight was below 100%) | rating-based | **+25 percentage points** |

**Excluded from the increase:** housing, education, vehicle, and loans secured by gold and gold jewellery.

Now put that exclusion list next to the FY26 growth table in §1.1 and the entire year explains itself. Gold at 50.4%, vehicles at 14–15%, housing at 9.4% — all excluded. Cards at 0.0% and personal loans slowed then rebounding — both included. **The book grew exactly where capital was cheap.**

## 6.3 The February 2025 partial reversal 

Two notifications, both dated 25 February 2025:

- **Microfinance loans in the nature of consumer credit** dropped from 125% back to **100%** — and to **75%** where they qualify for the regulatory retail portfolio. Applicable to outstanding as well as new loans, effective immediately on issue.
- **Bank exposure to NBFCs** had the November 2023 add-on of 25 percentage points removed, restoring the rating-based weights, effective 1 April 2025. Bank credit growth to NBFCs had fallen to 6.7% by December 2024 from about 15% a year earlier; the reversal was aimed squarely at that.

⚠️ **What did not change, and this is the correction most people get wrong:** the **125% on consumer credit and the 150% on bank credit card receivables were not rolled back.** The February 2025 action was about microfinance and about bank funding of NBFCs. `[VERIFY — no rollback of the consumer credit weight is visible to me as at 29 July 2026; confirm against the current Basel III Master Direction before stating it in a room.]`

**► SAY THIS**
> "The single best explanation of FY26's retail composition is the November 2023 risk-weight action. Consumer credit went to 125% and cards to 150%, but housing, education, vehicle and gold were explicitly excluded — and the book then grew almost exactly along that exclusion list. Gold 50%, vehicles 14 to 15%, housing 9%, cards zero. February 2025 partially reversed it for microfinance and for bank lending to NBFCs, but the consumer credit and card weights stand. So when I look at a growth number in this market, the first question I ask is what it costs in capital."
