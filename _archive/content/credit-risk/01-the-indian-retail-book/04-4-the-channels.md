# §4 · THE CHANNELS

Where a loan comes from predicts how it performs, more reliably than most bureau attributes. Four channels matter.

**1. Branch and direct.** Bank-owned, slowest, cheapest risk. Concentrated in large-ticket housing and auto.

**2. DSA — direct selling agents.** Commission-paid third-party sourcing. Dominant in mortgages and large personal loans. Well-understood risk: commission paid on disbursal creates an incentive to originate, not to be repaid. Every mature lender runs **DSA-level vintage analysis** and cuts off the tail. If you build one portfolio-monitoring artefact in your career, make it this one.

**3. Dealer and point-of-sale.** Auto, two-wheeler, consumer durables. The dealer is the origination point, which puts underwriting into a showroom with a customer waiting. Fast decisioning is the product. Dealer-level performance monitoring is to this channel what DSA-level monitoring is to the last.

**4. Digital, through LSPs.** The channel that produced the small-ticket personal loan market, and the one that has been most heavily re-regulated.

## 4.1 The digital lending rules 

The **RBI (Digital Lending) Directions, 2025**, issued 8 May 2025, consolidated and repealed the 2022 digital lending guidelines, the default loss guarantee framework, and the earlier circular on loans sourced over digital platforms. The architecture:

- Three roles: the **Regulated Entity** (the licensed lender), the **LSP** (lending service provider — customer acquisition, underwriting support, servicing, collections), and the **DLA** (digital lending app).
- **Responsibility is non-delegable.** Outsourcing to an LSP does not move the obligation. The RE owns conduct, data handling, grievance redressal and recovery-agent behaviour.
- Loan flows must move **directly between lender and borrower** — no pooling through an LSP account.
- **KFS — Key Facts Statement** — mandatory, per the April 2024 circular, with the all-in annualised cost disclosed.
- **DLA reporting to the RBI's CIMS portal** was required by 15 June 2025, creating a public directory of digital lenders.
- The **multi-lender LSP framework** took effect 1 November 2025: where an LSP presents offers from several lenders, it must show an unbiased view, use a consistent documented matching mechanism, and must not use dark patterns or preferentially promote one lender's product.
- **DLG** — default loss guarantee, previously FLDG — is permitted only with an LSP incorporated as a company, within a cap, and must not be structured to shift the credit decision to the unregulated party.

🔴 **The DLG trap, and it is a favourite.** A default loss guarantee does not transfer credit risk in any way that matters to the regulator. The RE remains the lender of record, must classify and provision the asset on its own books, and cannot use the guarantee to justify weaker underwriting. If someone describes an arrangement where "the fintech takes the first loss so our underwriting can be lighter," the correct response is that this is precisely the structure the framework exists to prevent.

## 4.2 Co-lending 

The **RBI (Co-Lending Arrangements) Directions, 2025**, issued 6 August 2025, in force from 1 January 2026, replaced the earlier priority-sector-focused framework with a general one.

- Applies to commercial banks (excluding SFBs, RRBs and local area banks), all-India financial institutions, and NBFCs including HFCs.
- **Each participating entity must retain a minimum 10% share** of the loan.
- Extends beyond priority sector — the earlier framework's main limitation.
- Does not apply to multiple banking, consortium or syndication arrangements.
- Where a co-lending arrangement is also digital, **both** frameworks apply.
- Full disclosure to the borrower through the KFS.
- Each participant may claim priority-sector status on its own share.

📘 **Why co-lending exists, in one line.** The bank has cheap capital and no reach; the NBFC has reach and expensive capital. Co-lending is the trade. The 10% retention floor exists so the originator keeps skin in the game and the arrangement does not become origination-for-fee with the credit risk fully passed through.
