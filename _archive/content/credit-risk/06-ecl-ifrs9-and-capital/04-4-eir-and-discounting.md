# §4 · EIR AND DISCOUNTING

## 4.1 What the Directions require 

📘 **Effective interest rate.** The rate that exactly discounts estimated future cash flows through the **expected life** of the instrument to its gross carrying amount, determined at **initial recognition**.

The Directions mandate:

- ECL for instruments **originated or invested in on or after 1 April 2027** is computed using the EIR determined at initial recognition.
- ECL for a **POCI** asset originated on or after that date uses the **credit-adjusted effective interest rate** — a rate that builds expected credit losses into the discount rate itself, because the asset was already impaired when acquired.
- **Opening ECL as at 1 April 2027**, computed on the 31 March 2027 balance sheet, may **at the bank's discretion** use the contractual interest rate as the discount factor as a transitional measure.
- **All such outstanding loans must migrate fully to the EIR regime by 31 March 2030.**

## 4.2 The two glide paths — closing Document 00's correction

This is where the correction flagged in Document 00 finally sits in its proper place, and it is worth stating cleanly because merging the two is a live error:

> **EIR migration runs to 31 March 2030.**
> **The provisioning and capital impact smoothing runs to 31 March 2031.**

Two different transitions, two different end dates, one year apart. They are not the same arrangement and they do not do the same job. §6 handles the second.

## 4.3 Why EIR matters beyond discounting

EIR is not only a discount rate — it is an **income recognition** method. Under it, fees, transaction costs and premiums or discounts are amortised over the expected life rather than recognised upfront.

⚠️ **Two consequences with real teeth:**

**Processing fees stop being day-one income.** A ₹5,000 processing fee on a five-year loan is spread across the loan's expected life rather than booked at disbursal. For a fast-growing lender writing large volumes of new business, this materially changes the **timing** of reported income — and the faster the growth, the larger the deferral. It does not change lifetime profitability; it changes when it appears.

**"Expected life" is a modelling input, not a contractual fact.** And Document 05 §4.4 already flagged the link: the pre-payment directions of January 2026 removed foreclosure friction for floating-rate retail, which **shortens expected life**. A shorter life means faster fee amortisation, a different EIR, and a smaller lifetime ECL. **One consumer-protection rule feeds into income recognition, ECL and pricing simultaneously** — and connecting those is the kind of cross-domain observation that distinguishes a candidate.
