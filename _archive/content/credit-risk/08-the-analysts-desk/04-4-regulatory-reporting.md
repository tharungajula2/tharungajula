# §4 · REGULATORY REPORTING

Distinct from management reporting, produced to a different definition, and reconciling the two is a real and recurring job.

## 4.1 The platforms 

| Platform | What it carries |
|:--|:--|
| **CIMS** — Centralised Information Management System | The RBI's returns platform. Statutory and supervisory returns migrated in phases through 2023–2025, with transition for various categories of regulated entity completed by May 2025. Also carries **digital lending app reporting** (Document 02 §4.1) |
| **DAKSH** | The RBI's advanced supervisory monitoring system. Compliance monitoring, **payment fraud reporting** through CPFIR since January 2023, and the platform on which **non-compliant credit information reporting** is flagged (Document 01 §7.2) |
| **CRILC** | Central Repository of Information on Large Credits. Large-exposure reporting, and where **red-flagged accounts** must be reported within seven days (Document 02 §4.2) |
| **CICs** | The four credit bureaus. From **1 July 2026**, four reference dates a month — 9th, 16th, 23rd and last day (Document 01 §7.2) |

⚠️ Returns run at every frequency from daily to annual, under the RBI Act, the Banking Regulation Act and FEMA.

## 4.2 The discipline that matters

🔴 **A regulatory number and a management number can legitimately differ, and you must be able to explain every difference.**

Common sources: borrower-level versus account-level classification; the treatment of technical write-offs (Document 02 §11.4); netting and set-off conventions; the exact regulatory definition of a product category against the internal one; and — from 2027 — **ECL against management's own expected loss**, which differ in the six ways enumerated in Document 06 §8.3.

✅ **Keep a standing bridge between the two, refreshed monthly.** It is unglamorous. It is also the document that saves you in a supervisory inspection, and the person who maintains it is the person the CRO calls when the RBI asks a question.

## 4.3 Pillar 3

Public disclosure of capital adequacy, risk exposures, risk-weighted assets by category, and — increasingly — ECL methodology, staging movement, scenario assumptions and overlays quantified separately (Document 06 §3.4).

📘 **Pillar 3 is written for competitors and analysts as much as for regulators**, which is why it is drafted with unusual care and why it is a genuinely useful source when you want to understand how a peer institution actually operates. **Reading two or three banks' Pillar 3 disclosures side by side is the single cheapest way to learn what real portfolio reporting looks like** — and it is free, current, and something almost no candidate does before an interview.
