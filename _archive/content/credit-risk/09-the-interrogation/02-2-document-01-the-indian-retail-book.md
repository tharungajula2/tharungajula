# §2 · DOCUMENT 01 — THE INDIAN RETAIL BOOK

**Give the shape of the Indian retail book in three sentences.**
₹170.2 lakh crore across roughly 688 million accounts, growing 16.6%. Home loans are a third of the value and 3% of the accounts. Gold, personal, cards and consumer durables are under a quarter of the value and more than two-thirds of the accounts.

**Why is "retail credit grew 16.6%" a nearly useless statement?**
Because within it gold grew 50.4% and credit cards grew 0.0%. The aggregate averages seven products with almost nothing in common.

**Break the gold loan growth into its causes.**
Four. Collateral revaluation — ticket size up 52% on flat volumes. The tiered LTV relaxation to 85% under ₹2.5 lakh. Reclassification of agri-gold into retail, about a third of the book. And genuine substitution away from unsecured. Only the last is borrower demand.

**Gold delinquency fell sharply. Why should you not be reassured?**
Falling delinquency during a bullion price rally is a collateral effect, not a credit-quality effect. A borrower whose pledged gold has appreciated will always find a way to repay or refinance rather than surrender it.

**What is the real gold loan exposure, then?**
A sustained price correction. Loans written near the peak sit closest to collateral value, trigger margin calls, and convert a benign book quickly. The RBI's June 2026 FSR flags exactly this, noting gold growth at a 42.4% CAGR driven by repeat borrowers.

**State the gold loan LTV tiers and the bullet repayment rule.**
85% up to ₹2.5 lakh, 80% from ₹2.5 to ₹5 lakh, 75% above ₹5 lakh. Bullet repayment consumption loans capped at 12 months, with LTV computed on the **total amount repayable at maturity**, not the amount disbursed.

**Why does that last clause bite so hard?**
It didn't change the cap; it changed what the cap is measured against. On a one-year bullet loan at 18%, a lender that could disburse ₹75,000 against ₹1,00,000 of gold can now disburse only about ₹63,500 — roughly a sixth less.

**Why did credit cards stop growing?**
Capital, not demand. The November 2023 action took bank card receivables to a 150% risk weight, the most expensive retail asset in the system. The marginal thin-file account stopped being written and acquisition moved to cross-sell inside the existing base.

**Personal loans: describe the two markets.**
Under ₹1 lakh is 88.6% of origination volume and 18.8% of value, written by NBFCs and fintechs. ₹10 lakh and above is 1.8% of volume and 35.7% of value, written by banks. PAR 91–180 is about 2.3% in the first and 0.33% in the second.

**What single relationship holds across every retail product?**
The ticket-size gradient — delinquency falls monotonically as ticket size rises. Home loans 2.85% at ₹5–35 lakh against 0.88% above ₹75 lakh; personal loans 2.27% under ₹1 lakh against 0.33% above ₹10 lakh.

**And what are the three possible causes of that gradient?**
Small tickets skew to thinner files and newer-to-credit borrowers; to lenders with faster, lighter underwriting; and to different use cases — a small loan is more likely to fund a shortfall than a plan.

**State the rule that explains the institutional map.**
Banks own value, NBFCs own volume. Which is why systemic risk lives in the bank book and borrower-level distress lives in the NBFC book.

**System GNPA is 1.8% and personal loan PAR 180+ is 5.3%. Reconcile them.**
Different universes — GNPA is banks only, bureau PAR includes NBFCs. Write-offs — GNPA is measured after write-off, bureau records often aren't. Different denominators, different definitions of the numerator, and product mix.

**Which of those five is the largest contributor?**
Write-offs.

**Give the microfinance cycle in four beats.**
Boom to March 2024, roughly ₹4.3 trillion. Stress surfacing mid-2024 on bureau-visible over-leverage. Seven consecutive quarters of contraction to about ₹3.25 trillion. The turn in Q4 FY26 — GLP up 3% sequentially, PAR 31–180 down to 2.0% from 6.3%.

**What are the five lessons it teaches?**
Over-leverage is a bureau-visible system condition, not an individual borrower failing. Write-offs are how a book "improves" — 14.1% of the opening FY25 book was written off. Contractions run longer than anyone forecasts. Self-regulation works and costs growth. And PAR 1–30 turns first, in both directions.

**Why did FY26's book grow exactly where it grew?**
The November 2023 risk-weight action excluded housing, education, vehicle and gold. Those are precisely the products that grew. The book grew where capital was cheap.

**What changed on 1 July 2026 and why does it matter?**
Bureau reporting moved from fortnightly to four reference dates a month — the 9th, 16th, 23rd and last day. It halves the loan-stacking window, which is the mechanism behind much small-ticket unsecured stress.

**And what does it break?**
Every velocity-based bureau attribute in every model built before that date. The same borrower behaviour now produces a different variable value.

**Three different housing loan numbers exist. Why?**
CRIF's ₹44.4 lakh crore is the whole bureau universe. RBI sectoral deployment is banks only, around ₹33.7 lakh crore. NHB datasets slice differently again. Whose book, which date, which definition, does it net off write-offs.
