# §9 · THE TRAPS

1. **Comparing a behavioural model's Gini with an application model's.** Different populations, different base rates. §1.1.
2. **Building behavioural variables on level rather than change.** Utilisation drift beats utilisation. §2.2.
3. **Ignoring endogeneity** when the model's own outputs drive limit actions. §2.3.
4. **Targeting default rather than cure** in a collections model. §3.1.
5. **Optimising collections propensity instead of uplift** — working accounts that would have cured anyway. §3.2.
6. **Reading a score gap linearly.** 80 points is roughly fifteen times the risk, not "somewhat better." §4.1.
7. **Presenting central tendency as observed** in a segment with no long run to observe. §4.2.
8. **Treating "the PD" as one number** across ECL and capital — and failing to be suspicious when the two are identical. §4.3.
9. **Multiplying a 12-month PD by tenor** to get a lifetime PD. Marginal hazards decline with seasoning. §4.4.
10. **Computing LGD undiscounted and cost-gross.** 30% versus 53% on identical cash flows. §5.1.
11. **Reporting mean LGD** on a bimodal distribution instead of modelling cure and severity separately. §5.2.
12. **Estimating LGD in benign conditions** and applying it to stressed ones, when collateral value and default probability are correlated. §5.3.
13. **Excluding incomplete workouts** without acknowledging the truncation bias toward good outcomes. §5.5.
14. **Treating DLG as reducing PD.** It reduces LGD, it depletes, and it carries counterparty risk. §5.6.
15. **Quoting a single CCF** for a revolving portfolio rather than estimating by utilisation band. §6.3.
16. **Saying ECL is a small extension of Basel EL.** Five differences. §7.1.
17. **Over-investing modelling effort where the prudential floor binds anyway.** §7.2.
18. **Reporting a PD backtest pass in a high-quality grade** as if it were evidence. §8.1.
