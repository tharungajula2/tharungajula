# PART V — KEEPING CURRENT

*The rulebook moves. Here is the routine that keeps you from citing a dead instrument.*

**Daily, two minutes.** RBI "Notifications" page. Everything binding appears here first.

**Bi-monthly.** The Monetary Policy Statement, specifically the Statement on Developmental and Regulatory Policies at the end. **This is where instruments are announced before they exist.**

**Half-yearly.** The Financial Stability Report. Read the asset quality and macro stress-test chapters properly.

**Annually.** Report on Trend and Progress. And re-verify every instrument name in Tier 1 against the Master Directions index.

**Event-driven.** When a major Direction drops: read the RBI press release, then the instrument itself, then two rating-agency impact notes, then one Big Four clause-level analysis. **In that order.** Reading the commentary first anchors you to someone else's reading.

### The three habits that separate a practitioner from a reader

**Cite the paragraph.** Not the instrument, not the news article — the paragraph. It takes ten extra seconds and it changes how people treat your input.

**Read the feedback statement.** When RBI issues final Directions with a Statement on Feedback Received, that document tells you which industry objections were accepted and which were rejected. **It is the closest thing you get to the regulator explaining its reasoning**, and it is where you learn what RBI actually cares about.

**Track the gap between draft and final.** <cite index="38-1">One commentator's read of the final ECL guidelines was that a closer look reveals hardly any changes from the draft norms</cite> — while <cite index="34-1">KPMG identified select changes and additional clarifications on prudential floors, POCI assets and EIR determination.</cite> **Both are defensible readings of the same document**, and noticing that two credible sources disagree is more useful than picking one. It tells you the changes were real but narrow.
