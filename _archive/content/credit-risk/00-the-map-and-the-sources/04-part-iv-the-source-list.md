# PART IV — THE SOURCE LIST

*The core deliverable of this document. Organised by how you'd actually use them.*

## TIER 1 — The instruments you must have read

These are the primary rulebook. If you cite anything in this set, cite one of these.

**Reserve Bank of India (Commercial Banks – Income Recognition, Asset Classification and Provisioning) Directions, 2025** · RBI · **[IN FORCE]** until 31 March 2027
> The operative provisioning regime today. Asset classification, SMA and NPA definitions, provisioning rates, upgrade and restructuring rules. Document 02 is built on this. *Note: this replaced the long-standing Master Circular in the October 2025 consolidation.*

**Reserve Bank of India (Commercial Banks – Asset Classification, Provisioning and Income Recognition) Directions, 2026** · RBI · notified 27 Apr 2026 · **[FROM 01-04-2027]**
> The ECL framework. Staging, SICR, prudential floors, EIR, POCI, governance. Document 03. Read alongside the **Statement on Feedback Received** issued with it, which tells you which draft provisions moved and why — that document is disproportionately informative and almost nobody reads it.

**Reserve Bank of India (Commercial Banks – Capital Charge for Credit Risk – Standardised Approach) Directions, 2026** · RBI · notified 27 Apr 2026 · **[FROM 01-04-2027]**
> Risk weights, regulatory retail portfolio, real estate, CCF, credit risk mitigation. Document 04. Also read its feedback annex.

**Master Circular – Basel III Capital Regulations** · RBI · **[IN FORCE]** until superseded
> The current capital framework, including the existing regulatory retail portfolio rules at para 5.9. <cite index="63-1">Para 5.9.1 sets the four qualifying criteria for the 75% regulatory retail portfolio; para 5.9.2 excludes consumer credit including personal loans.</cite> [VERIFY — whether this remains a "Master Circular" or has been reissued as Directions in the consolidation; check the RBI Master Directions index.]

**Ind AS 109 – Financial Instruments** · MCA, Companies (Indian Accounting Standards) Rules · **[IN FORCE]** for NBFCs/HFCs
> The accounting standard behind ECL. Classification and measurement, the impairment model, EIR. Read with **Ind AS 107** for disclosures.

**RBI circular on Implementation of Indian Accounting Standards — NBFC prudential floor** · RBI · **[IN FORCE]**
> The instrument requiring NBFCs to hold the higher of Ind AS ECL and IRACP provisioning, with the shortfall transferred to an impairment reserve. [VERIFY — exact current instrument name and whether it survived the consolidation intact. This is the single most-cited NBFC provisioning rule and you must have the correct reference.]

**Credit Information Companies (Regulation) Act, 2005** and the **CIC Rules and Regulations, 2006** · Parliament / RBI · **[IN FORCE]**
> The statutory basis for credit bureaus, data furnishing obligations, consumer rights, dispute resolution.

**Reserve Bank of India (Credit Information Reporting) Directions, 2025** · RBI · **[IN FORCE]**, with amended provisions applicable **from 1 July 2026**
> Fortnightly reporting, data quality, correction timelines, customer notification. <cite index="80-1">RBI deferred the amended reporting directions for CICs to 1 July 2026 from the earlier 1 April 2026, and after industry feedback removed the requirement to report incremental accounts on the 28th of every month and extended various timelines.</cite> [VERIFY — that the 1 July 2026 date held and the amended provisions are now live; this is within weeks of the as-at date of this document.]

**Reserve Bank of India (Digital Lending) Directions, 2025** · RBI · **[IN FORCE]**
> <cite index="74-1">Issued 8 May 2025, consolidating and replacing the 2022 Digital Lending Guidelines and the 2023 Default Loss Guarantee Guidelines.</cite> <cite index="71-1">Effective 8 May 2025, except multi-lender arrangement provisions from 1 November 2025 and DLA reporting requirements from 15 June 2025.</cite> Document 08.

**Reserve Bank of India (Co-Lending Arrangements) Directions, 2025** · RBI · **[IN FORCE]** from 1 Jan 2026
> <cite index="72-1">Issued 6 August 2025, effective 1 January 2026, repealing the November 2020 co-lending guidelines.</cite> <cite index="70-1">Eliminates the discretionary CLM-2 model, requires each partner to retain a minimum 10% of every individual loan — down from 20% under the PSL co-lending regime — and introduces borrower-level asset classification synchronisation requiring information sharing by the next working day when either lender classifies an exposure as SMA or NPA.</cite> That last requirement is a genuine operational problem and a good thing to have an opinion about.

**Reserve Bank of India (Lending Against Gold and Silver Collateral) Directions, 2025** · RBI · **[IN FORCE]**, amendments effective 1 Oct 2025
> <cite index="75-1">Amendments clarify restrictions on loans for purchase of gold and loans against primary gold or silver, and fold earlier circulars into the Master Direction framework.</cite>

**Master Direction – Reserve Bank of India (Regulatory Framework for Microfinance Loans) Directions, 2022** · RBI · **[IN FORCE]**
> Household income definition, the 50% repayment-capacity cap, pricing and conduct. Essential if you touch MFI or SFB portfolios. [VERIFY — whether reissued or amended in the consolidation.]

**Master Directions – Reserve Bank of India (Priority Sector Lending – Targets and Classification) Directions, 2025** · RBI · **[IN FORCE]**
> PSL categories, targets, and the classification rules that shape a great deal of Indian retail origination.

**Reserve Bank of India (Commercial Banks – Resolution of Stressed Assets) Directions, 2025** · RBI · **[IN FORCE]**
> Restructuring and resolution framework. <cite index="77-1">Referenced by the 2026 ACPIR Directions for the definitions of DCCO and financial closure.</cite>

**Reserve Bank of India (Transfer of Loan Exposures) Directions** · RBI · **[IN FORCE]**
> Loan sale, direct assignment, securitisation-adjacent transfers. <cite index="70-1">Cherry-picking and selective loan purchase arrangements must comply with TLE rather than the co-lending provisions.</cite> [VERIFY — current year designation after consolidation.]

## TIER 2 — RBI publications you track on a schedule

These are how you know what's actually happening, as opposed to what the rules say.

**Financial Stability Report** · RBI · half-yearly, typically June and December
> The single best source on Indian retail credit stress. Contains the macro stress test results, sectoral asset quality, unsecured retail delinquency trends, and the systemic risk survey. **If you read one thing per half-year, read this.**

**Report on Trend and Progress of Banking in India** · RBI · annual, typically December
> The definitive statistical picture of the banking system. Asset quality by bank group, credit growth by sector, provisioning coverage.

**Monetary Policy Statement and Governor's Statement** · RBI · bi-monthly
> Where major regulatory intentions are announced before they become drafts. <cite index="42-1">The ECL and Basel III implementation dates were announced in the October 2025 policy statement, months before the drafts.</cite> **Reading these is how you see instruments coming.**

**Statistical Tables Relating to Banks in India** and **Basic Statistical Returns** · RBI · annual
> Granular credit data by occupation, population group, and bank group.

**Sectoral Deployment of Bank Credit** · RBI · monthly
> Retail credit growth by sub-segment. This is where you see personal loan and credit card growth turning before it appears anywhere else.

**RBI Bulletin** · monthly
> Contains research articles that frequently preview policy thinking.

**Master Directions index on rbi.org.in** · continuously updated
> After the October 2025 consolidation, this index *is* the rulebook. Bookmark it. **Check it before citing any instrument by name.**

## TIER 3 — International reference

You need these to explain how India compares, which you will be asked.

**Basel III: Finalising post-crisis reforms** · BCBS · December 2017
> <cite index="45-1">The framework RBI's 2026 capital Directions implement.</cite> The source document for the revised Standardised Approach.

**CRE (Calculation of RWA for credit risk)** · BCBS consolidated framework
> The live, browsable version of the Basel credit risk standard. More usable than the 2017 PDF.

**IFRS 9 Financial Instruments** · IASB
> Ind AS 109's parent. Useful for reading global bank disclosures and for understanding where Ind AS deliberately diverges.

**ASC 326 / CECL** · FASB
> The US alternative. Worth one page of understanding: lifetime ECL on everything from day one, no staging. Relevant if you target global roles later.

**SR 11-7 / the 2026 revised interagency Model Risk Management guidance** · Federal Reserve, OCC, FDIC
> The reference model-governance framework globally. **Note: SR 11-7 was superseded by revised interagency guidance in 2026** — a lot of material still cites SR 11-7 as current. [VERIFY — the exact superseding letter numbers and issue date; sources differ between April and May 2026.]

## TIER 4 — Industry and data sources

**TransUnion CIBIL** — Credit Market Indicator, industry insights reports. The most-cited retail credit data in India.
**CRIF High Mark** — *How India Lends* and portfolio reports, strong on microfinance and rural.
**Experian India** and **Equifax India** — the other two CICs.
**CRISIL, ICRA, CARE, India Ratings** — sector reports on banks and NBFCs. <cite index="40-1">ICRA assessed the ECL framework's capitalisation impact on 30 April 2026.</cite> These agencies publish impact analyses within days of major RBI instruments and they are usually the fastest credible read.
**Big Four India thought leadership** — KPMG, PwC, Deloitte, EY, plus Uniqus and Grant Thornton Bharat, publish detailed clause-level analyses of new Directions. **Excellent for orientation, not a substitute for the instrument.**

## TIER 5 — The professional layer

**IIBF** certifications (Certified Credit Professional, Risk in Financial Services) — recognised in Indian banking HR.
**PRM / FRM** (GARP) — globally recognised, and the FRM syllabus maps closely onto Documents 04–07.
**Vinod Kothari Consultants** — consistently the sharpest free clause-level commentary on Indian financial-sector regulation. <cite index="70-1">Their co-lending analysis includes genuine critique — for example, calling the per-lender CIC reporting requirement unnecessary and undesirable.</cite> That kind of reasoned dissent is rare and worth learning from.
