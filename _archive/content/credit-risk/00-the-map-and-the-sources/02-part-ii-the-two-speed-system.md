# PART II — THE TWO-SPEED SYSTEM

*This is the single most useful frame for an Indian retail credit risk practitioner, and it's the thing that makes NBFC and bank roles feel like different jobs.*

## 2.1 The split, stated plainly

**NBFCs and HFCs have been running expected credit loss since FY2018–19.** They report under **Ind AS 109**, which is India's convergence with IFRS 9. Three stages, SICR, lifetime ECL — all of it, for seven years now.

**Banks have not.** Banks report under RBI's prudential IRACP regime — an incurred-loss, rule-based framework — and are **not** on Ind AS. <cite index="40-1">Larger NBFCs already work this way under Ind AS 109 since 2018–19, and India is now applying this logic to banks directly.</cite>

**So today, two lenders can hold the identical retail loan and provision it completely differently.** An NBFC personal loan at 45 DPD may already be Stage 2 with lifetime ECL. The same loan at a bank is a standard asset carrying a general provision.

## 2.2 Why this matters for your career, not just your knowledge

**If you work at an NBFC today, you are already doing the work banks are about to start.** That is a genuine, sellable asset — a candidate who has actually built or run an Ind AS 109 staging engine is scarce and about to become scarcer, because every bank in India is hiring for exactly that between now and April 2027.

**If you work at a bank today, you are living in the regime that is about to be replaced.** Your IRACP knowledge stays valuable — it doesn't vanish in 2027, and the transition itself requires people who understand both — but the growth is on the ECL side.

**The interview line that lands, and it is true:** *"India runs a two-speed provisioning system right now. NBFCs have been on Ind AS 109 since FY19; banks are on prudential IRACP until FY28. From 1 April 2027 they converge — but not completely, because RBI has layered prudential floors on the bank framework that Ind AS 109 doesn't impose on NBFCs. So even after convergence, a bank and an NBFC holding the same loan won't necessarily provision it identically."*

## 2.3 The NBFC prudential layer

NBFCs carry **both** — Ind AS 109 for accounting *and* RBI's prudential norms. Where the two produce different numbers, RBI requires the higher to be met, with the difference booked to a specific reserve. The mechanism is covered in Document 03; note here only that **"NBFCs are on Ind AS so they don't have IRACP" is wrong**, and it's a common mistake.

NBFCs are also layered under RBI's **Scale Based Regulation** framework — Base, Middle, Upper and Top Layer — with obligations scaling by layer. Which layer an NBFC sits in determines a lot about the risk function you'd be joining.
