## § 8.7 — Pilot to production

<cite index="31-1">The failure consistently happens during the transition from pilot to production.</cite> Document 07 covered the *technical* half. This is the organisational half, and it's the larger one.

### Why pilots die after they succeed

**The pilot's success conditions aren't production's.** Your pilot ran on 200 files with an engaged underwriter who knew he was in a pilot. Production is 40,000 files and thirty-eight people who were told about it in an email.

**The pilot had you.** Every friction point in the pilot was resolved by a person who was paid to be there full time. Production has a ticket queue.

**Nobody owns it.** The pilot had a sponsor, a budget line, and a champion. Production needs an owner in the operating org, an on-call rotation, a budget that recurs, and a place in someone's roadmap. **These are not technical artifacts and they do not create themselves.**

**And the sponsor moved.** Priya gets promoted, reorganised, or reassigned, and the new person inherits a project they didn't choose. This happens more often than not on an eight-month engagement.

### The conversion checklist

Start this in month four, not month seven.

**A named production owner** in the operating organisation, not in the project. Someone whose job it is when you're gone.

**A recurring budget line**, not project funding. Pilots are funded as projects and die when the project closes.

**An on-call rotation that includes their people**, with at least one incident handled by them before you leave. The § 7.17 drill, run for real.

**A runbook their team wrote**, or at minimum edited. Documentation you wrote alone is documentation nobody has read.

**A place in the roadmap.** If the system needs a change in month nine and there's no team with capacity to make it, it will freeze and then rot.

**And the success sentence from § 8.3, measured and reported.** The number that says whether this worked, published to the person who agreed to it.

**THE DEPLOYMENT LENS.** The sponsor-change risk is the one to actively manage, because you can't prevent it.

**Write down why every significant decision was made, as you make them.** Not a design document — a *decision log*. One line each: the date, the decision, the alternatives considered, and the reason.

*"2026-04-14: memo drafts only, no decision rendering. Alternative: full recommendation. Reason: adverse-action obligation under ECOA/Reg B; validation timeline; Whitfield and Nwosu both required it."*

When a new sponsor arrives in month six and asks "why doesn't it just make the decision?", that log answers in ten seconds instead of costing you a week of relitigating. **It is the cheapest insurance in the entire engagement and it takes ninety seconds a week.**
