## § 8.11 — Stakeholders

You met five people at Meridian in Document 00. Here is the general shape, because it repeats.

**The sponsor** (Priya) — has the budget, wants the outcome, is not technical, and can cancel you. Needs: progress, money, and no surprises. **The failure mode is going quiet.** A sponsor who hasn't heard from you in three weeks assumes something is wrong, and they're often right. Short, regular, honest updates — including bad news early — are the entire relationship.

**The domain expert** (Dan) — knows the actual business, is professionally skeptical, and is usually correct. Needs: to be taken seriously and not to have his standards lowered. **He is your best reviewer and the most common person to alienate**, by treating his objections as resistance rather than information.

**The governance function** (Marcus, Adaeze, Rina) — exists to find problems with you. Needs: evidence, reproducibility, and to not be surprised. **The counterintuitive truth: they are your allies.** They tell you what will actually be required, which is the most valuable information available. Bring them findings before they ask, and propose controls you're excluded from operating.

**The user** (Tom) — whose work changes, who was not consulted, and who decides whether this succeeds. Needs: to not be threatened, to be heard, and for his corrections to visibly matter. **The whole adoption question lives here.**

**The platform team** — owns the infrastructure you deploy into, is busy, and did not ask for your project. Needs: you to follow their conventions and not create operational burden. **Asking "show me a service you're happy with" and copying it is worth more than any amount of goodwill.**

### The two you'll miss

**The person nobody mentions who knows how the old system works.** Left the org chart, still answers questions, is the reason anything functions. Find them in week one.

**And the person whose job the project makes worse.** There is usually one — a team lead measured on something your system disrupts, an ops group whose workaround you're replacing. **They will not object openly; they will simply not help**, and their non-help is often the thing that stalls you in month five. Find them, and find out what it costs them.
