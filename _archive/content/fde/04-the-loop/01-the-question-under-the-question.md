## The question under the question

Tom's question was not about accuracy. Re-read it: *"What happens on the ones you don't catch?"*

He is asking about **the system's behaviour when it is wrong and doesn't know it.** Which is a question about architecture, not quality — about what the system does with its own uncertainty, who gets told, and what stops a bad output from becoming a bad decision.

Everything in this document is machinery for that question. And it arrives with a temptation attached, which is why the first section is a warning rather than a technique.

The temptation is **autonomy**. You now have retrieval that works. The natural next thought is: let the model decide what to retrieve, let it call tools, let it investigate, let it loop until satisfied. That is genuinely more capable. It is also, at a regulated lender, mostly wrong — and knowing exactly *how much* of it is right is the judgement this document exists to install.
