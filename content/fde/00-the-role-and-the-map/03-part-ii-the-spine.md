## PART II — THE SPINE

Every document in this set advances one deployment through one arc. The arc has seven movements, and it is the same arc in every FDE job you will ever hold.

**1. Discovery.** Find out what the customer actually does, as opposed to what they told the salesperson. Find the real process, the real people, the real data, the real politics.

**2. Scoping.** Choose the narrowest slice that produces visible value and is actually shippable. Say no to four things. This movement kills more deployments through cowardice than any technical failure.

**3. The demo.** Build the 20%. Make it real enough to be believed, honest enough to survive questions.

**4. Integration archaeology.** Discover that the data is in a 1990s system, half the fields are free text, the schema doc is wrong, and the person who knew how it worked left in 2019. This is where the 80% begins.

**5. Hardening.** Evals, security, auth, monitoring, cost control, failure handling. The unglamorous work that converts a demo into a system a regulated institution will run.

**6. Production and adoption.** Ship it. Watch nobody use it. Find out why. Fix that. Adoption is a separate problem from deployment and it is not your product manager's job while you are on site.

**7. Handover and pattern-back.** Make it survivable without you, and carry what you learned back to the product team. <cite index="6-1">The best FDEs are the eyes of the product team — if an FDE's work isn't influencing what gets built next, the role is being misused.</cite>

Documents 01 through 08 walk this arc. Nothing is taught out of sequence.
