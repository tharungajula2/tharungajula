## § 8.2 — A loop, not a pipeline

The most useful reframe in this document, and it changes how you plan.

<cite index="26-1">Forward deployed engineering is a delivery model in which a customer-embedded, production-owning engineering team identifies a high-value operational problem, designs the AI-native workflow, builds or adapts the technical system, deploys it into the real environment, measures adoption, and turns what works into reusable delivery patterns. The role does not stop at discovery, recommendation, or prototype.</cite>

<cite index="26-1">It is an operating model that combines discovery, engineering, deployment, adoption, and feedback into one loop</cite> — and the shift, stated four ways: <cite index="26-1">from handoff to ownership, from requirements to redesign, from pilot to production, from one-off delivery to repeatable capability.</cite>

**Compare that to how a normal project is planned.** Requirements, then build, then test, then deploy, then hand off. Each phase ends and the next begins, and adoption is somebody else's problem.

In this model, **adoption feeds back into discovery** — you learn what to build next by watching what people actually do with what you built. **And deployment feeds back into your company's product**, which is the loop's outer ring.

The practical consequence: **if your engagement plan has "deployment" as the last box, it's wrong.** Deployment is roughly the midpoint. Everything after it — adoption, handover, pattern extraction — is where the value is decided, and it's routinely allocated zero time.

**Here's the failure statistic that should organise your planning.** <cite index="31-1">Research has found that companies using identical AI models see dramatically different results, and the failure consistently happens during the transition from pilot to production — precisely the moment that proximity to the operation becomes most valuable and is most often missing.</cite>

**The technology is not the variable.** Same models, wildly different outcomes, and the difference is who was in the room when the pilot met reality.
