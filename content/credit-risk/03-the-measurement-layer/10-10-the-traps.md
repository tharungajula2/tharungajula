# §10 · THE TRAPS

1. **Quoting a snapshot delinquency ratio as evidence of underwriting quality.** It confounds quality, seasoning and growth dilution. §1.
2. **Reading improving delinquency in a fast-growing book as improvement.** The arithmetic in §1 shows 4.0% becoming 2.7% with nothing changed.
3. **Reporting a ratio without its numerator in rupees.** §2.2.
4. **Mixing value-weighted and account-weighted delinquency** without saying which. §2.1.
5. **Plotting vintage curves against calendar date instead of months on book.** This destroys the entire point of the technique.
6. **Reading vintage level without reading shape.** Worse from MOB 2 is fraud or sourcing; worse from MOB 6 is credit. §3.3.
7. **Presenting a portfolio-level vintage chart.** The aggregate always conceals a bad tail of channels. §3.4.
8. **Chaining a transition matrix and calling it a forecast.** It is a nowcast under current conditions. §4.5.
9. **Assuming Markov behaviour across delinquency history.** A repeat visitor to a bucket behaves worse than a first-timer. §4.5.
10. **Explaining a flat deep bucket as borrower behaviour.** It is write-off policy. §5.3.
11. **Computing LGD on undiscounted, cost-gross recoveries.** §6.2.
12. **Treating EAD as the current balance on a revolving line.** §6.3.
13. **Running PSI without CSI.** Offsetting variable shifts leave PSI quiet. §8.3.
14. **Tightening cut-offs in response to a data-cadence artefact.** §8.4.
