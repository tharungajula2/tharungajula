# §10 · THE FIRST NINETY DAYS

If you take a seat in a retail credit risk function, this is the order.

**Weeks 1–2 — the map.** What products, what volumes, what channels, what systems. Read the last four board risk committee packs; they tell you what the institution worries about. Read the credit policy.

**Weeks 3–4 — the data.** Do monthly snapshots exist and how far back? Does the mart reconcile to the ledger? Where are origination-date attributes stored? Can you get borrower-level aggregation? **Write down what does not exist. That list is your first contribution.**

**Weeks 5–8 — reproduce.** Take last month's published pack and rebuild every number yourself. You will find discrepancies. Understanding them teaches you the data model faster than any documentation, and finding one real error in your first two months establishes you permanently.

**Weeks 9–12 — one finding.** Run the §6 playbook on the segment with the worst trend. Produce one finding, quantified in rupees, with a recommendation. **One finding that changes a decision is worth more than three months of faultless pack production**, and it is what people will remember when they describe you to someone else.

✅ **And the thing to resist:** the urge to build a model in month one. You do not yet know the data, the definitions, the policy history or the mix. A model built in that state will be wrong in ways you cannot see, and it will be the thing you are known for.
