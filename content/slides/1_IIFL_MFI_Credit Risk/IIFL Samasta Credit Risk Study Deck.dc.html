A · ORIENTATIONCOMPRESSION · 02/99

The whole subject, in one paragraph

Retail credit risk replaces a judgement about one borrower with a measurement of many. You define an event — in India, the non-performing asset, at borrower level, 90 days overdue — freeze what is known about an account, wait a stated period for the verdict, fit a model that ranks and a calibration that levels, then multiply the resulting probability by a severity and an exposure. Three consumers treat that number differently: pricing wants today's odds, provisioning wants today's odds under a forecast and then compares the answer against a regulatory floor, and capital in India ignores the model and reads a risk weight from a table. In a microfinance book one thing changes, and it changes everything downstream: the lender cannot repossess anything, so its only security is the borrower's belief that repaying buys the next loan. Risk is therefore geographic and social rather than individual, every metric that matters is a leading indicator of that belief breaking, and provisioning runs as two parallel systems — Ind AS 109 ECL and the RBI IRACP floor — rather than one.

What an NBFC-MFI Is, and Why Funding Structure Drives Everything

India's lending system has banks on one side and Non-Banking Financial Companies on the other. NBFCs lend but cannot take deposits from the public the way a bank does. They fund themselves by borrowing from banks, issuing bonds, and selling down loan pools.

An NBFC-MFI is a specialised category: an NBFC whose business is microfinance loans — small, collateral-free loans to low-income households, historically to women, historically in groups. RBI regulates the category tightly because the borrowers are the least able to absorb a mistake.

A bank funds loans with deposits, which are sticky and cheap. An NBFC-MFI funds loans with borrowings, which are neither. When asset quality deteriorates, banks get nervous, funding lines tighten, cost of funds rises, and the MFI has to shrink its book — which shrinks income while fixed branch costs stay put.

Deterioration is not just a P&L event for an MFI. It is a liquidity and growth event. That is why the risk MIS I would produce is read by more people than it would be at a bank: business, finance, treasury, the rating agencies, and the lending banks.

B · THE BUSINESSCONCEPT · 04/99

The Joint Liability Group, and the Promise of the Next Loan

The structure

Joint Liability Group. Five to ten women from the same locality form a group. Each takes her own loan. There is no collateral. The group provides a social guarantee: members know each other, meet weekly or fortnightly with a loan officer at a centre meeting, and there is peer pressure — sometimes peer payment — when one member falls behind.

The mechanism that actually matters

The mechanism people quote is that joint liability makes borrowers repay. The mechanism that actually operates is the promise of the next loan. A borrower in cycle 3 holds an implicit credit line that grows with each repaid cycle. She repays because losing access to formal credit means going back to a moneylender at 60–120% a year.

The two consequences

Loan cycle is the single most predictive variable in MFI credit risk.

Not income. Not ticket size. A cycle-1 borrower behaves completely differently from a cycle-4 borrower, because cycle 1 has nothing accumulated to lose yet.

Anything that breaks the promise of the next loan breaks repayment across a whole area at once.

If borrowers believe the lender is going to stop lending — a political announcement, a rumour, an ordinance, a competitor exiting — repayment collapses not because they cannot pay but because the incentive is gone. This is the mechanism behind almost every MFI crisis in India, including 2024–25.

RBI Microfinance Directions, 2022

Master Direction — Reserve Bank of India (Regulatory Framework for Microfinance Loans) Directions, 2022. Issued 14 March 2022, effective 1 April 2022. Amended since.

This replaced the old NBFC-MFI-specific regime with a borrower-centric, lender-agnostic framework: the same rules bind banks, small finance banks, NBFCs and NBFC-MFIs when they make a microfinance loan.

The definition

A microfinance loan is a collateral-free loan to a household with annual household income up to ₹3,00,000. "Household" means people living under one roof and sharing a kitchen, regardless of legal relationship.

MFIN (Microfinance Institutions Network) is the RBI-recognised Self-Regulatory Organisation for the sector. Sa-Dhan is the other. Guardrails are not RBI regulation — they are industry-agreed norms — but members follow them, bureaus enforce them operationally, and rating agencies treat them as binding.

Why 60 instead of 90 is genuinely clever

90 DPD is the NPA threshold. Cutting off at 60 means you stop lending to a borrower before she becomes an NPA anywhere in the system, rather than after. It is a systemic early-warning rule, not a credit rule.

What it did to the business

Rejection rates jumped. Origination volumes fell sharply — Q3FY25 originations down over 40% year-on-year. Roughly 4 lakh borrowers exited formal microfinance entirely. That is the price of the medicine.

D · THE LOSS ENGINEFORMULA · 14/99

The loss equation. Everything else is an input to it, an output of it, or a check on it.

EL = PD × LGD × EAD

EL

Expected loss, in currency. The mean loss over many accounts like this one. Per year or per lifetime, depending on the horizon of PD.

PD

Probability of default, between 0 and 1, for a stated horizon — usually 12 months. Floored at 0.03% in Indian ECL: no exposure may be modelled as riskless.

LGD

Loss given default. The fraction of exposure not recovered, after costs and after discounting to the default date.

EAD

Exposure at default. The amount outstanding at the moment default occurs — not the amount outstanding now.

Interpretation. The word expected carries the weight. Expected loss is a mean and no individual account experiences it — an account either defaults or it does not. A loan with 4% PD and 70% LGD on ₹5,00,000 has an expected loss of ₹14,000, but the realised loss is nil 96% of the time and about ₹3,50,000 the rest. ₹14,000 is a price, not a prediction.

Consequence. Expected loss is a cost of doing business, so it belongs in the price and in provisions. Capital exists for the variation around the mean — unexpected loss. Confusing the two is the most expensive error in the subject: a lender would either under-provide and over-capitalise, or the reverse, and could not explain either number to a supervisor.

D · THE LOSS ENGINECONCEPT · 15/99

The Three Terms, Term by Term

PD — the odds

A probability of a defined event over a stated horizon, estimated from a model fitted on historical accounts and their verdicts. Two horizons matter: 12-month and lifetime.

It is the only parameter whose ranking drives approve or decline, which is why conservatism is kept out of it.

Where it comes from: application scorecard at origination, behavioural scorecard thereafter, a hazard curve for lifetime.

LGD — the severity

One minus the recovery rate, where recovery is measured properly: all cash received after default, net of the costs of collecting it, discounted back to the default date. Each qualification removes several percentage points of illusion.

Post-default interest charged but never paid is not recovery.

PD gets the attention; LGD gets the money. A secured book's expected loss is driven more by how long enforcement takes and what the security fetches than by how many borrowers stop paying.

EAD — the exposure

On amortising loans the balance falls, so EAD is read off the amortisation schedule. On revolving products the balance rises, because a borrower heading into distress draws on remaining headroom.

EAD = B + CCF × (L − B)

B drawn balance · L committed limit · L − B undrawn headroom · CCF credit conversion factor, the fraction of headroom expected to be drawn before default.

Where conservatism should live

Padding PD changes who gets sanctioned, because PD drives the cut-off. Padding LGD raises expected loss roughly proportionally and leaves the ranking untouched. Padding EAD raises everything and distorts limit-setting. So: keep PD as unbiased as the data allows and put conservatism in LGD and EAD.

The regulator made the same choice

Where a lender cannot reliably estimate its own severity, the Indian backstop LGD is 65% on the secured portion and 70% on the unsecured portion, dropping to 30% only for cash, gold, central and state government securities, LIC policies, Kisan Vikas Patra and National Savings Certificates. Deliberately harsh numbers that penalise not knowing.

What "Default" Means, and Why the Definition Is Load-Bearing

PD is not a probability of loss. It is a probability of a defined event, and in India that event has a single legal definition — the non-performing asset — which the accounting framework has adopted verbatim.

FacilityNPA trigger
Term loanInterest or principal overdue more than 90 days
Credit cardMinimum amount due unpaid for 90 days from the statement payment due date
Cash credit / overdraft"Out of order" — three limbs, any one sufficient: outstanding above sanctioned limit or drawing power for 90 continuous days; or no credits for 90 days; or credits insufficient to cover interest debited in the previous 90 days
Crop loansTwo crop seasons overdue for short-duration crops, one for long-duration

The subtle limb is the third: an account can be inside its limit, receiving money every month, and still be non-performing because the money is not even covering interest.

Two Indian rules make classification harsher

  • Borrower level, not facility level. If any one exposure to a borrower is NPA, every exposure to that borrower is NPA, including facilities that have never missed a payment. A single defaulted card contaminates the same customer's housing loan.
  • The day-end process is definitive. An account is flagged overdue in the day-end run for the due date and becomes NPA on the calendar date whose day-end run finds it 90 days overdue. No month-end smoothing, no discretion about the date.

Trap — "the borrower paid something, so the account improves"

It does not. An NPA upgrades only when the entire arrears of interest and principal are repaid, across all facilities of the borrower. Borrower and co-borrower are jointly and severally liable. This makes cure a discrete, all-or-nothing event — which is precisely why the distribution of outcomes after default is sharply bimodal and why any LGD model built on average behaviour misleads.

Default is not loss. A borrower who reaches 90 days and then clears the arrears in full has defaulted and produced no loss. On secured retail, cures commonly run at a third to a half of defaults. That is why LGD is modelled rather than assumed to be one.

Observation Point and Outcome Window

A credit model is trained on a photograph of the past taken at one moment and a verdict delivered some months later. Almost every serious modelling error is an error in choosing those two moments.

Observation point — the date at which you freeze everything known about an account: application data, bureau data, balances, payment history.
Outcome window — a fixed period after it, during which you watch for the default event. Default inside the window makes the account a bad; never defaulting makes it a good. The model can only ever predict over the window length it was trained on.

Window length is a trade-off, not a detail. Twelve months is standard for unsecured personal loans and cards: it captures most of the emergence and keeps the development sample recent. Housing needs 24 months or more, because those defaults arrive slowly. But a long window pushes the most recent usable observation point that far into the past, so the model inherits that period's economy, prices and competitive environment.

Is the window long enough? Plot the cumulative bad rate for a mature vintage against months-on-book. At the chosen length the curve should have flattened enough that six more months would add less than about 10% to the cumulative bad rate. Still climbing steeply at the cut-off means the model is trained on early defaulters only — it will rank fraud and severe distress well and ordinary deterioration poorly.

Indeterminates

Accounts neither clearly good nor clearly bad in the window — typically those that reached SMA-1 or SMA-2 but never became NPA, or closed early for non-credit reasons. Usually excluded: counting them as goods teaches the model that near-misses are fine, counting them as bads teaches it that one late payment is fatal. Exclusion is not free — the model is then trained on a sharpened version of reality and its odds need recalibrating onto the full population. Above roughly 5–10% of the sample, exclusion deserves a written justification.

Three sampling decisions

  • Draw at a point in time or across a band of adjacent months, not pooled across years, or the model fits the economy rather than the borrower.
  • Bads are rare. A 4% bad rate means 96 rows of noise per 4 rows of signal, so bads are often kept in full while goods are sampled down. This shifts the intercept and requires a correction.
  • Sample accounts, not applications, unless the model's job is to score applications.
β₀corr = β̂₀ − ln(ρ₁ / ρ₀)

ρ₁ sampling fraction applied to bads, usually 1 · ρ₀ sampling fraction applied to goods, e.g. 0.1 if one in ten kept. Only the intercept moves; slope coefficients are unaffected, which is why the correction is safe.

Seasoning: Vintages and Months on Book

Read a portfolio by calendar month and the delinquency rate rises and falls and the economy takes the blame. Read it by vintage — grouping accounts by the month they were disbursed and measuring performance by months on book (MOB) — and a characteristic shape appears.

  • Bad rates start near zero. Nobody is 90 days overdue in month one.
  • They climb steeply to a peak between roughly MOB 9 and 18 for unsecured products.
  • They then flatten, as the surviving population self-selects toward good payers.

A calendar-time chart mixes vintages at different points on that hump, so a book growing at 30% a year looks better than it is — it is full of young accounts that have not had time to fail.

The economy is real, but seasoning explains more month-to-month movement than the economy does. Separating the two is the first job of portfolio analysis.

Two definitions to keep separate

CohortAny group sharing an attribute, followed over time. Branch, state, product, cycle, ticket band, sourcing officer.
VintageThe specific cohort that matters most: grouped by month of disbursement, plotted against MOB rather than calendar time.

Point-in-time vs through-the-cycle

PIT estimates default probability given everything known today, including where the economy stands; it rises in downturns. TTC estimates a long-run average for a grade, deliberately insensitive to the cycle. Neither is more correct — they answer different questions. The ECL framework requires PIT. No real model is purely one or the other.

Procyclicality, the trade-off

PIT gives provisions that are accurate and volatile. TTC gives provisions that are stable and, in a downturn, too small. Every regime picks a point on this line and adds machinery to compensate: probability-weighted scenarios on the accounting side, a countercyclical buffer on the capital side. No setting is simultaneously accurate, stable and simple.

The censored feedback loop

Outcomes are observed only on borrowers who were sanctioned, so the data that trains tomorrow's model was selected by today's. This single fact generates reject inference, range restriction and the winner's curse — three separate problems with one cause.

E · SCORECARDSFORMULA · 20/99

Weight of Evidence and Information Value

WoEi = ln[ (gi/G) ÷ (bi/B) ]
IV = Σ (gi/G − bi/B) × WoEi

gi, bi goods and bads in bin i · G, B total goods and total bads. A positive WoE means the bin holds proportionally more goods than the book average, so it is safer than average. Either sign convention works provided one is used throughout. Every IV term is non-negative, because the difference in proportions and the log-ratio always share a sign.

Interpretation. WoE replaces a raw variable's bin with a single number encoding its riskiness, which makes the relationship linear by construction, caps outliers inside their bins, gives missing values their own coefficient, and turns coefficient sign checks into a free diagnostic. IV summarises how much a variable separates goods from bads before any other variable is considered.

Binning is two stages. Fine classing cuts into twenty or so narrow bins and plots each bin's bad rate. Coarse classing merges adjacent bins until the pattern is monotonic or at least defensible, every bin holds at least about 5% of the sample, and each bin's bad rate differs meaningfully from its neighbours'. Missing values usually become their own bin — "declined to state income" is a fact about the applicant.

Reject Inference, and the Only Unbiased Fix

The training sample contains only accounts that were sanctioned, but the model will be applied to everyone who applies. If yesterday's cut-off declined the worst 40% of applicants, the relationship between characteristics and default has been observed only on the surviving 60%, and it is being extrapolated into a region where it was never measured.

The three common methods

ParcellingScore the rejects with a known-good / known-bad model, then assign bad flags randomly in proportion to the expected bad rate at each score, usually inflated by a factor of two to four
AugmentationReweight accepts so each stands in for the rejects that resemble it
ExtrapolationExtend the bad-rate curve beyond the cut-off

All three share the same defect.

Trap — "reject inference fixes selection bias"

It cannot. The outcomes of declined applicants were never observed, and no algorithm creates data. What reject inference does is make an assumption about the rejected region explicit, consistent and auditable, so the model's behaviour below the cut-off follows a stated rule rather than an accident of extrapolation.

The only unbiased remedy

Deliberately sanction a small random sample of applicants who would otherwise be declined, and observe what happens. Those swap-in test accounts are the only unbiased evidence about the declined population. Portfolios that fund them are the ones whose cut-offs can be moved with confidence.

Logistic regression versus gradient boosting

A gradient-boosted ensemble usually beats a well-built scorecard on the development sample by 2 to 5 Gini points, and often by less than that out of time. The costs are concrete: the model cannot be read as points, monotonicity must be imposed as an explicit constraint, reasons for rejection must be reconstructed after the fact, and independent validation takes longer.

Discrimination versus Calibration — Two Separable Jobs

Discrimination is rank ordering: given two accounts, does the model put the one that defaults below the one that does not? It survives any monotonic transformation of the score.

Calibration is level: of all the accounts assigned a 4% PD, do about 4% actually default? It is a property of the mapping from score to probability, and can be destroyed or repaired without touching the ranking at all.

A sanction cut-off needs only discrimination. Pricing and provisioning need calibration, because they multiply the PD by real money. A model with Gini 0.5 and PDs uniformly half of reality makes good sanctioning decisions and under-provisions by half.

The asymmetry that drives monitoring design. A fall in Gini is a redevelopment trigger. A drift in observed default rate is usually a recalibration one. That is why rank-order statistics and calibration statistics are reported separately.

Measuring a Model: AUC, Gini, KS, Hosmer-Lemeshow

Gini = 2 × AUC − 1
KS = maxs │Fbad(s) − Fgood(s)│

AUC is the probability that a randomly chosen bad scores worse than a randomly chosen good — the area under the ROC curve, which plots cumulative bads captured against cumulative goods rejected at every cut-off. A useless model gives the diagonal; a perfect one the top-left corner. Gini rescales it so useless is 0 and perfect is 1.

KS is the largest vertical gap between the cumulative bad and cumulative good distributions. It answers a narrower question than Gini — how good is the single best cut-off — which is why it survives in operational reporting despite Gini being the better summary. Conventionally quoted ×100.

Hosmer-Lemeshow groups accounts into deciles of predicted probability and compares observed against expected defaults with a chi-square statistic. It tests calibration, not ranking. A low p-value rejects the hypothesis that predicted and observed agree.

The Hosmer-Lemeshow sample-size property

The chi-square statistic scales with n. At a few hundred thousand observations it will reject on differences that are economically immaterial. This is why HL is read alongside the observed-versus-expected decile table rather than as a pass/fail gate, and why intercept recalibration can improve fit visibly while the p-value stays small.

PAR, Precisely — and PAR versus the Arrears Ratio

PAR X = Σ outstanding principal of all loans with any instalment overdue > X days ÷ Σ total outstanding principal

The two things to be precise about

  • The numerator is the entire outstanding balance of the affected loan, not just the overdue amount. This is exactly what distinguishes PAR from a simple arrears ratio.
  • PAR 31–180 is the sector-standard band MFIN reports, deliberately excluding the 180+ book, which is effectively written off.

PAR versus arrears ratio

PARCounts the whole balance of the affected loan
Arrears ratioCounts only the overdue amount

On a 24-month loan two instalments down, the arrears ratio sees two instalments and PAR sees the whole remaining principal. PAR is the more conservative and the more useful, because a borrower two instalments behind on an unsecured collateral-free loan puts the entire balance at risk, not two instalments of it.

The four denominator choices

Principal outstanding, total outstanding including accrued interest, disbursed amount, or AUM including sold-down pools. All four are defensible. They give different answers. Pick one per report and state it.

PAR answers "how sick is the book right now". It does not answer "is our underwriting getting better or worse". Only vintage answers that.

Roll Rates and Chained Forward Flow

roll rate (b → b+1) at t =
balance in bucket b+1 at t+1 that was in b at t
÷ balance in bucket b at t
forward flow = Π (consecutive roll rates)

A retail book is a flow system. Every month each account sits in a bucket and moves to another. The matrix of those movements is the transition matrix; the probabilities of falling one bucket further behind are the roll rates. Multiply consecutive roll rates to project eventual charge-off from today's current book — that is chained forward flow.

Roll rates are the earliest honest signal in the system. They respond within a month or two of a change in underwriting or the economy, while the stock of NPAs takes at least three months to reflect the same change, and write-offs take longer still.

Two related definitions

Flow rateThe same object as a roll rate, read from the perspective of accounts leaving rather than arriving
Cure rateThe proportion moving back toward standard — in India, clearing the entire arrears across all facilities. Not the complement of the roll rate, because accounts can also stay put, close, or be written off

Cure rate is the most volatile item in the pack. It responds to collections staffing, to seasonal payment patterns around harvests and festivals, and to any change in restructuring policy — so an unexplained rise in cure rate is more often an operational change than an improvement in customer health.

Stage 1, Stage 2, Stage 3

Every in-scope instrument sits in one of three stages, determined by how its credit risk has moved since initial recognition — not by its absolute level of risk.

Stage 1

No significant increase in credit risk since initial recognition.

Allowance = expected losses from defaults occurring in the next 12 months.

Interest recognised on the gross carrying amount. In capital terms, 12-month ECL allowances count as general provisions in Tier II, within limits.

Stage 2

Significant increase in credit risk, but not credit-impaired.

Allowance = expected losses over the instrument's remaining lifetime.

Applied at facility level. Interest still recognised on the gross carrying amount.

Stage 3

Credit-impaired, which in India means it meets the NPA definition.

Allowance = lifetime expected losses, with interest recognised only on receipt.

Applied at borrower level — one impaired facility drags every other facility of that borrower into Stage 3.

The two frameworks run side by side

The ECL framework does not replace NPA classification. "Default" in the ECL sense is defined as NPA status, so an entity reports both a stage and an asset classification for the same account. Anything built on the definition of default survives a regime change. Anything built on the old provisioning arithmetic does not.

Ind AS 109 does not define default

It requires consistency with internal credit risk management, and RBI guidance says align it with the regulatory definition. So Stage 3 is aligned to 90 DPD. Accounts past 90 days not treated as impaired need Audit Committee approval with documented rationale.

SICR and the 30-DPD Backstop

The trigger from Stage 1 to Stage 2 is a significant increase in credit risk since initial recognition — a change in default risk over the remaining life, not an absolute level of risk. Two exposures with identical PDs can sit in different stages.

How it is implemented

  • Documented quantitative rules — rating downgrades by a stated number of notches, a defined increase in loan pricing, or a defined deterioration in the macroeconomic outlook. Whichever parameter is chosen must be defined precisely and applied consistently.
  • A rebuttable presumption of SICR at 30 days past due. For revolving facilities, the presumption arises when the outstanding stays above the sanctioned limit or drawing power continuously for up to 60 days.
  • Rebuttal is permitted only with reasonable and supportable information, must be documented and board-approved, and may not be applied mechanically. In practice the 30-DPD backstop is rarely rebutted.

The ECL methodology itself must be board-approved, documented, with the sensitivity of assumptions recorded. RBI points entities to the BCBS guidance on ECL as reference.

Trap — "Stage 2 is a warning; the money is all in Stage 3"

On a personal loan the allowance more than doubles on migration to Stage 2, and on a longer-dated product the multiple runs to five or ten times, for an account whose underlying risk may have moved only modestly.

The consequence: a small deterioration in a macroeconomic forecast can push a large block of accounts across the SICR boundary at once and produce a charge far larger than the change in expected defaults justifies. This cliff effect is intended rather than accidental.

Which is why reported provision movements must be decomposed first

In this order: stage transfers → scenario weights and forecast changes → model and assumption updates → volume and mix → write-off and recovery timing → any floor that started binding. Only the residual is genuine credit deterioration, and most of the time it is smaller than the headline. Decompose before you diagnose.

Lifetime PD from a Discrete Hazard Curve

PDcum(t) = 1 − Πi=1..t (1 − hi)

Construction

Estimate a monthly hazard hi — the conditional probability of defaulting in month i given survival to its start — from observed defaults by months-on-book. Take the running product of survivals. One minus that product is cumulative PD to month t.

Account-level scaling. A portfolio curve is scaled to an individual account by the multiplicative ratio of the account's 12-month PD to the portfolio's 12-month PD, evaluated at the account's remaining term, floored at 1 month and capped at original tenure. This keeps cumulative PDs monotonically non-decreasing and bounded in [0, 1].

Why lifetime exceeds 12-month by more than the horizon ratio

  • Marginal PDs compound over survival — each period adds to the cumulative total.
  • Exposure is still outstanding in later periods.
  • Later-period conditional default rates are usually higher than the first period's, because of the seasoning hump.

A 60-Month Curve against a 12–24 Month MFI Curve

What changes when tenure is 12–24 months

  • The curve is 12–24 buckets, not 60. My method scales cleanly; the horizon and the shape change.

Lifetime ECL ≈ 12-month ECL for much of the book, because remaining tenure is often under twelve months.

So the Stage 1 → Stage 2 cliff effect that dominates IFRS 9 discussion in mortgage books — where the multiple runs to five or ten times — is much smaller in microfinance. Saying that shows I have thought rather than memorised.

It also cuts the other way: with a short curve, the provision is dominated by staging accuracy and LGD, not by lifetime-PD sophistication. That is a reason to spend effort on SICR triggers and cure modelling rather than on a longer hazard curve.

Scenarios Are Probability-Weighted on Outputs, Never on Inputs

Losses are convex in the economy: a two-point deterioration costs more than twice what a one-point deterioration costs. So a single central forecast systematically understates expected loss.

ECL( mean scenario )  <  mean( ECL of each scenario )

The framework therefore requires multiple scenarios, each representing a relationship between the ECL components and the relevant macroeconomic variables, with probability weights determined by the entity using historical experience and expert judgement under its governance framework, and reviewed at a documented frequency.

The weighting is applied to the resulting ECLs. Averaging the scenarios first and then computing ECL once is wrong, and it is wrong in a predictable direction — it always under-provides.

The linkage is built with satellite models

Regressions of an observed portfolio quantity — the default rate, the roll rate, the recovery rate — on lagged macroeconomic variables. Lags are long: deterioration in employment typically leads unsecured defaults by two to four quarters.

Model sensitivity versus model stability

A tightly fitted satellite model responds strongly to the scenario, which supervisors like, and strongly to noise in the forecast, which produces provision volatility with no informational content. A loosely fitted one responds smoothly and can be accused of ignoring the stress. There is no resolution inside the model. Report the sensitivity explicitly — rupees of provision per point of the driver — so the choice is visible rather than buried.

The Indian record is thin, and one episode is unusable

The 2020 moratorium and the accompanying standstill on asset classification suspended the very ageing process that defines default. Days-past-due series from that period record policy, not borrower behaviour. Any model calibrated across it without explicit adjustment will misstate both the level and the timing of stress. Document the treatment rather than letting the artefact pass as data.

The Impairment Reserve Mechanism

Impairment Reserve = max( 0,  IRACP provision − Ind AS 109 ECL )

The four rules that make it bite

  • Appropriated from post-tax profit — it is not a P&L charge and it does not run through the impairment line.
  • It does not count toward regulatory capital.
  • It cannot be reduced from GNPA to compute NNPA. Netting it would flatter the headline asset-quality ratio, so it is explicitly barred.
  • It cannot be withdrawn without prior permission from RBI's Department of Supervision.

Why the design is genuinely elegant, and worth saying so

Ind AS 109 gives management judgement — staging, scenarios, overlays. IRACP gives a hard, rule-based, un-gameable floor. Management Overlays — and Why the Release Criteria Must Be Written First

Management overlays get treated as a fudge, and in a normal book that's often fair. In a microfinance book during something like Karnataka, a modelled ECL is structurally wrong, because the model was fitted on a world where the incentive to repay held. The honest treatment is an explicit, board-approved geographic overlay with the quantum, the trigger and the release criteria documented — so the judgement is visible and auditable rather than smuggled into an assumption. What I'd care about is that the release criteria are written down at the same time as the overlay, not afterwards.

Most candidates get this wrong by treating overlays as cheating. Auditors expect them; what auditors do not accept is an overlay with no exit condition.

How to set one — the judgement answer

  1. Quantify from analogous historical events where the record permits.
  2. Size it against exposure in the affected geography, not against the whole book.
  3. Get board approval, and disclose it.
  4. Write the release criteria at the same time as the overlay — the trigger, the quantum and the exit, all three.

An overlay without an exit condition becomes permanent, and then indefensible. That is the whole reason the release criteria are written first: afterwards, releasing it looks like earnings management, and holding it looks like a hidden reserve.

Where an overlay is not the answer

If the population has changed structurally rather than temporarily — as it did on 1 April 2025 — the correct response is redevelopment, not an overlay. An overlay patches a level; it cannot fix a model applied to a different population.

Average LGD Describes No Individual Account

Retail LGD is bimodal. Most defaults resolve either near-fully — a cure, or a secured sale that clears the debt — or near-totally, an unsecured write-off with a token recovery.

Two Indian rules amplify it. Upgradation requires clearing the entire arrears across all facilities, so cure is all-or-nothing. And an exposure can be commercially secured and regulatorily unsecured at the same time — land, buildings, plant, machinery and current assets are excluded from the eligible-collateral list used to define the secured portion of an NPA for risk weighting.

A model that gets the average right by predicting 30% for everything, when reality is 60% of accounts at 5% and 40% at 67%, will price and provision individual segments wrongly in both directions. Check the distribution, not just the mean.

"It's the correct number for that book, and it's exactly why I used a hurdle model — a mean of 0.93 describes almost no individual account, because the distribution is bimodal and the mean sits in the empty middle."

The Two-Stage Hurdle Architecture

LGD = 1 − P(recovery) × E[ RR │ recovery > 0 ]

Why the split is necessary

Recovery rates exhibit severe point-mass concentrations at zero and at one, which breaks the distributional assumptions of standard linear and beta regression. A hurdle model separates the two questions instead of averaging over them.

Stage 1Logistic regression for P(has_recovery = 1) — recovery incidence
Stage 2Gradient boosting regressor for the recovery rate, conditional on recovery being non-zero — recovery magnitude, where non-linear interactions live

The full LGD definition, for completeness

LGD = 1 − [ Σt (Rt − Ct) / (1+d)t ] ÷ EAD

Rt cash recovered in period t · Ct direct and allocated indirect costs of recovery · d discount rate, the effective interest rate under the ECL framework · EAD exposure at the moment of default. Cash arrives over years, so discounting matters; legal fees, valuation, auction costs and agency commissions are real.

The MFI Inversion: LGD Is Cure-Driven, Not Recovery-Driven

My LGD is a US charge-off world, where recovery means a collections agency chasing after write-off. In microfinance most of the "recovery" is cure. A borrower who goes 60 DPD during a bad harvest and comes back at 120 days is not a recovery — she is a cure, and the loan performs to term.

MFI LGD is therefore dominated by three terms

P(cure)The probability the account returns to performing. High in normal conditions, collapses in a geographic event
Post-write-off recoveryGenuinely low. There is no collateral, and legal recourse for a ₹40,000 unsecured loan is uneconomic
Guarantee coverageCGSMFI-covered exposures have materially lower effective LGD. The model must know which loans are covered — and must recompute the allowance each time the guarantee is invoked and its cover shrinks
LGD = (1 − Pcure) × (1 − RRnoncure) × (1 − guarantee_cover)

How I restructure my two-stage model for this world

Stage 1 becomes a logistic classifier for P(cure within N months), conditioned on DPD at observation, loan cycle, group status, branch PAR and district stress flags.

Stage 2 stays a recovery-magnitude model for non-cures — but expect it to be thin, and expect the honest answer to be a near-flat high LGD with the real variance sitting in stage one.

The architecture I used transfers, but the economics invert. My hurdle model splits P(any recovery) from recovery magnitude, because 52% of my defaults recovered nothing. In an MFI book I'd keep the two-stage shape but make stage one P(cure) rather than P(recovery) — because in this book the money is in accounts that come back, not in what you collect after charge-off. And I'd carry guarantee coverage as an explicit term, because CGSMFI changes effective LGD on a chunk of the book.

Downturn LGD

Capital and prudence want LGD conditional on an adverse state. Pairing a stressed PD with an average LGD understates the tail, because PD and LGD worsen together.

In secured lending: property prices fall, so defaults rise and recoveries shrink simultaneously. In an MFI book the same correlation is sharper still — cure rates collapse precisely when default rates spike, which is the definition of a geographic event.

My project's +8 pp downturn add-on is the right instinct even though the IRB wrapper around it is not applicable. It moved RWA from $2.295B to $2.454B, risk weight from 125.63% to 134.35%, and minimum total capital by $12.74M.

The three validation questions, in order

  1. Is the model conceptually sound? Do the variables make sense, is the target well defined, is the estimation appropriate?
  2. Does ongoing monitoring show it still works?
  3. Does outcomes analysis show its predictions matching reality on data it has never seen?

A model can pass the third and fail the first, and that combination is the dangerous one — it works until the regime it silently depends on changes.

When to retire rather than recalibrate

  • A sustained fall in discrimination that recalibration cannot address.
  • A population shift large enough that the development sample no longer resembles the applicant flow.
  • A change in the product or in the classification rules that breaks the target.
  • Age. Most retail scorecards are redeveloped on a two-to-four-year cycle — not because they expire on a date, but because accumulated drift of population, product and economy reliably exceeds tolerance in that window.

The three-tier structure the Indian rules prescribe

Model owners in the front line develop, implement and use models, accountable for approval, validation and performance. Risk management and compliance oversees independent validation and enforces limits. Internal audit provides objective assurance on both and reports to the board or audit committee. Above all three, a board committee including the CFO and CRO challenges implementation strategy, ensures data integrity across the ECL lifecycle, and guarantees the independence of validation.

Three obligations are where implementations usually fail: a model inventory cataloguing every model with owners, tier, upstream and downstream dependencies and validation status; risk-based tiering so validation intensity follows materiality rather than convenience; and a documented lifecycle — development, pre-implementation validation, implementation, monitoring, independent validation, recalibration or retirement — with a prospectus stating methodology, limitations and initial validation outcomes.

The Four Traps That Produce Wrong Answers Confidently

1 · Range restriction

Tightening the cut-off removes the worst accounts, which were the easiest to rank, so sanctioned-book Gini falls mechanically with no degradation in the model.

Fix: measure discrimination on the through-the-door population, or interpret sanctioned-book Gini only alongside the approval rate that produced it. Never compare Gini across periods with different approval rates.

2 · Coincident ratios

Any ratio whose numerator and denominator come from different moments in time lies — in both directions, depending on whether the book is growing or shrinking.

Fix: lag the denominator by the emergence period, or move to vintage analysis. And strip out technical write-offs, which shrink the numerator with no cash arriving.

3 · Leakage

A variable that is a consequence of the default rather than a predictor of it. The tell is an information value above roughly 0.50, or a development-to-out-of-time performance gap much larger than expected.

Fix: treat a suspiciously good variable as guilty until proven innocent, and treat a large development gap as more informative than the winner. Verify zero identifier overlap across partitions explicitly, in code.

4 · Definition drift

The default flag used to build the model, the flag used to classify NPAs for supervisory reporting, and the flag that drives Stage 3 must agree. A model trained on facility-level 90 DPD will systematically under-count defaults against the borrower-level regulatory definition.

Fix — the standing check: run all three flags over the same month of data. Pass condition: the three populations agree to within a few percent, and every disagreement traces to a documented rule rather than a surprise.

Where Conservatism Lives, and What Documentation Has to Carry

Where conservatism should live, and why

In LGD and EAD, keeping PD as unbiased as the data allows — so the ranking used for approve and decline stays undistorted while provisions and capital remain prudent.

The reasoning is mechanical, not aesthetic. PD drives the cut-off, so padding it changes who gets credit. LGD rarely varies much within a product, so padding it raises expected loss roughly proportionally and leaves the ranking intact. Padding EAD raises everything and distorts limit-setting.

Backtesting, stated as a standing item

Compare ECL predicted against realised loss by vintage, quarterly. For LGD, take a cohort of defaults old enough to be fully resolved and compare predicted LGD at default with realised LGD discounted at the same rate. Pass condition: the mean sits inside the stated confidence range and — separately — the distribution is reproduced, not just the mean.

For declines, the Fair Practices Code obligation is that the principal reasons are conveyed in writing. Pass condition: reasons are specific — "utilisation of existing revolving limits is too high", not "credit score insufficient" — tied to actual model inputs, and reproducible from the stored score record months later. A model whose reasons must be reconstructed by re-running a current version against an old application has failed this even if the reasons sound plausible.

    * 100.0

      OVER (PARTITION BY branch_id)

c · The real roll rate — a self-join across two month-ends

WITH b AS (
  SELECT loan_id, snapshot_date,
         principal_outstanding,
         CASE
           WHEN dpd = 0    THEN 0
           WHEN dpd <= 30  THEN 1
           WHEN dpd <= 60  THEN 2
           WHEN dpd <= 90  THEN 3
           ELSE 4 END AS bkt
  FROM loan_snapshot
  WHERE snapshot_date IN
    ('2026-06-30','2026-07-31')
)
SELECT p.bkt AS from_bucket,
       c.bkt AS to_bucket,
       SUM(p.principal_outstanding)
         AS pos_moved,
       SUM(p.principal_outstanding)
         * 100.0
       / SUM(SUM(p.principal_outstanding))
         OVER (PARTITION BY p.bkt)
         AS roll_pct
FROM b p
JOIN b c
  ON p.loan_id = c.loan_id
 AND p.snapshot_date = '2026-06-30'
 AND c.snapshot_date = '2026-07-31'
GROUP BY p.bkt, c.bkt
ORDER BY p.bkt, c.bkt;

d · Vintage curve — cumulative ever-30+ by MOB

WITH obs AS (
  SELECT l.loan_id,
    DATE_TRUNC('month',
      l.disbursement_date) AS vintage,
    DATEDIFF('month',
      l.disbursement_date,
      s.snapshot_date)     AS mob,
    MAX(CASE WHEN s.dpd > 30
          THEN 1 ELSE 0 END)
      OVER (PARTITION BY l.loan_id
            ORDER BY s.snapshot_date
            ROWS UNBOUNDED PRECEDING)
      AS ever_30
  FROM loans l
  JOIN loan_snapshot s
    ON s.loan_id = l.loan_id
)
SELECT vintage, mob,
       AVG(ever_30::float)
         AS cum_ever_30_rate,
       COUNT(DISTINCT loan_id)
         AS accounts
FROM obs
WHERE mob BETWEEN 0 AND 24
GROUP BY vintage, mob
ORDER BY vintage, mob;

Say while writing: "ROWS UNBOUNDED PRECEDING is what makes it ever-30 rather than currently-30 — once a loan has touched the bucket it stays flagged, which is the whole point of a vintage curve."

The Boundary Architecture That Clears Governance

The five rules, stated as rules

  1. Personal data never crosses the boundary. Aggregate first, always.
  2. The LLM never makes the decision. It drafts, summarises, or writes code. A human and a documented model make decisions.
