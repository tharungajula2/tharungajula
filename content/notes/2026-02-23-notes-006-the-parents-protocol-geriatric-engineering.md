## 2) Module 1: The Foundation (Muscle & Bones)

### The Glitch

“They’re just getting old / weak.”

### The Mechanism: Sarcopenia (Muscle Poverty)

Aging doesn’t just reduce “strength.” It specifically hits **fast-contracting Type II fibers**—the ones that save you when you trip. With age, fast fibers shrink more than slow fibers, reducing power, speed, and balance recovery.

And the cascade is brutal:

**Type II atrophy → slower reflex → unstable gait → fall → fracture → bedrest → infection/clot/delirium → mortality**

Falls are not “small accidents.” Globally, they’re a major cause of death and disability in older adults.

In India, hip fracture outcomes can be lethal: a large Indian cohort showed **~19% 1-year mortality overall**, and **~33% in those >80**.

### The Patch: “Muscle = Independence Pension Fund”

If your parent keeps their legs and back strong, they keep:

* Toilet independence
* Bath independence
* Walking independence
* Dignity

#### Patch A — **Minimum Effective Resistance Protocol (Home Version)**

**Goal:** 2–4 days/week, 20–35 minutes.
**Rule:** *No heroics. Only consistency.*

**Level 1 (Untrained / fearful):**

* Chair stands (sit-to-stand): 3 sets of 5–10
* Wall push-ups: 3 × 8–12
* Supported calf raises: 3 × 10–15
* Marching in place (hold table): 2 minutes
* Balance hold: stand near wall, 3 × 20–40 seconds

**Level 2 (Functional):**

* Slow squats to chair: 3 × 8–12
* Resistance band row: 3 × 10–15
* Step-ups (low step): 3 × 8–10 each leg
* Farmer carry (two water bottles): 4 × 20–40 steps
* Tandem walk (heel-to-toe): 5 passes

**Non-negotiable:** **legs + hips** (sit-to-stand, step-ups)
That’s where falls are decided.

#### Patch B — Protein (Yes, even for them)

Older muscle is “anabolically resistant”: it needs a stronger signal to maintain tissue.

Evidence-based guidance for older adults commonly lands around **~1.0–1.2 g protein/kg/day** (higher if active, lower only in specific kidney disease contexts under medical guidance).

**Indian-friendly protein patches:**

* Dahi / Greek yogurt / paneer
* Dal + curd (not dal alone)
* Chana / rajma / soy
* Eggs (if allowed)
* Whey (if appetite is low)

**Debug the chai-biscuit breakfast:**

* Replace biscuit with: eggs / sprouts chaat / dahi bowl / paneer bhurji
* Same ritual. Better kernel.

### The Bone Bank: Osteoporosis (Silent until it snaps)

In India, osteoporosis is common; studies show **~1 in 3 postmenopausal women** can have osteoporosis on DXA screening.

**DEXA (DXA) is the diagnostic gold standard** for bone mineral density.

#### Bone Patch Protocol

* **Women 60+ (or earlier if risk):** ask for a DXA scan plan
* Don’t run on “calcium tablets only”
* Bone meds (when needed) are **not supplements**—they’re actual anti-fracture tools (doctor decision)

**Reality:** A fracture is not a bone problem. It becomes a **life problem**.

## 3) Module 2: The Audit (Data & Medicines)

### The Glitch

* The “plastic bag” medical record archive
* The “medicine box” with 15 mixed strips
* “Doctor ne diya hai” (no one remembers why)

### The Mechanism: Polypharmacy + Prescribing Cascades

Polypharmacy is commonly defined as **5+ meds**, and it correlates with harm (falls, interactions, hospitalizations).

The killer pattern is the **prescribing cascade**:

> A drug causes a side-effect → it’s mistaken as a new disease → another drug is added → the stack grows.

And in elders, “side effects” often look like:

* dizziness
* confusion
* constipation
* sleepiness
* low appetite
* falls

…so families call it “aging,” while it’s actually **medication drift**.

Also: common painkillers (NSAIDs) can **raise blood pressure** and **blunt antihypertensive effect** in some patients.
And certain combinations (ACE/ARB + diuretic + NSAID) can increase kidney injury risk, especially in older adults.

### The Patch

#### Patch A — The Digital Timeline (Master File)

You are building a **single source of truth**.

**Folder structure (copy-paste logic):**

* `00_IDs_Insurance`
* `01_Diagnoses_Timeline`
* `02_Medications_Current`
* `03_Labs_Chronological`
* `04_Imaging_ECG_Echo`
* `05_Discharge_Summaries`
* `06_Allergies_Implants`

**File naming convention:**
`YYYY-MM-DD__Hospital__Department__What.pdf`
Example: `2024-11-02__ApolloGreams__Cardio__Echo.pdf`

**Bonus patch:** create an **ABHA number** (14-digit) to plug into India’s digital health ecosystem (consent-based sharing).

#### Patch B — The Brown Bag Review (Once a Year)

Once a year, you do a **full medicine audit**.

**Protocol:**

1. Put **every pill** (including OTC, ayurvedic, “vitamins”) into a bag
2. Sit with **one** physician (ideally family/internal medicine/geriatric-friendly)
3. Ask one question:

> **“What can we stop?”**

**Your audit sheet (make this in Notes):**

* Medicine name (brand + generic)
* Dose + timing
* Who started it
* Why it exists (original diagnosis)
* Start date
* Side effects noticed
* “Still needed?” (Y/N/Unknown)

**You’re hunting for:**

* duplicates (two brands, same molecule)
* meds without a clear “why”
* sedatives causing falls
* BP meds causing dizziness
* diabetes meds causing lows
* painkillers used like candy

#### Patch C — The Annual Geriatric Panel (Baseline Telemetry)

Do not “feel-based” manage a 70-year-old.
You need data.

**Annual core (typical):**

* CBC
* Fasting glucose + HbA1c
* Lipids
* Kidney function (urea/creatinine/eGFR)
* Electrolytes (Na/K)
* Liver enzymes
* TSH
* Vitamin D (interpret with clinician)
* Urine routine (+ microalbumin if diabetic)
* BP log (home readings)
* Vision/hearing check
* Gait & balance screen

(Your doctor may add B12, ferritin, ECG, etc based on context.)

## 4) Module 3: The Fortress (Home Engineering)

### The Glitch

“A fall in the bathroom.”

### The Mechanism: Light + Friction + Balance

Aging eyes literally need more photons:
People in their 60s may need **~3× more light** for comfortable reading than those in their 20s.

Aging balance needs:

* predictable surfaces
* high contrast
* stable grab points
* clutter-free paths

Indian bathrooms often ship with:

* wet tiles
* no grab bars
* dim night lighting
* bucket/mug chaos
* slippery footwear

That’s a **hostile environment**.

### The Patch: Room-by-Room Fortress Audit

#### Bedroom (3AM is the danger zone)

* Motion-sensor night light (bed → bathroom path)
* Phone + torch within reach
* Clear floor (no “charging cable trap”)
* Bed height: feet touch floor fully

#### Bathroom (highest ROI upgrades)

* **Grab bars** (real ones; not towel rods)
* Anti-slip mat + anti-slip treatment
* Shower chair if needed
* Footwear: non-slip bathroom sandals only
* Dry-floor rule: mop/wipe after every bath

#### Living room

* Remove loose rugs (or tape them down hard)
* High contrast on step edges
* No low furniture surprises

> Falls are preventable by improving environments + training + medication review.

### Emergency Protocol: The Golden Hour (India Edition)

Calling **108** is not a strategy. It’s one tool in your pipeline.

India’s emergency medical response is uneven; a Maharashtra analysis cited extreme delays (e.g., only **~7%** of head injury cases reaching hospital within the “golden hour,” and **~80%** of trauma patients not receiving care within the first hour).

**Golden Hour Patch Kit:**

* Decide **2 hospitals** in advance (primary + backup)
* Save routes in Google Maps (day & night)
* Keep a **Grab Bag** by the door:

  * IDs, insurance card, latest med list
  * last discharge summary
  * allergies + implants note
  * power bank + charging cable
  * cash + one spare set of clothes

#### Will

A Will is not pessimism.
It’s **anti-chaos**.

#### Living Will (Advance Medical Directive)

India’s Supreme Court recognized and later simplified the “living will / advance directive” process to make it workable; key changes include attestation before a **notary or gazetted officer** (instead of requiring a magistrate’s countersign), with **two witnesses**.

**Why this matters:**
When the ventilator question arrives, you don’t want to make a life-defining decision inside a noisy corridor at 2AM.

**You want the decision to already exist.**

#### Insurance Trap Checklist (read the fine print)

* Co-pay percentage for 60+ claims
* Room rent sub-limits (and proportionate deductions)
* Waiting periods for pre-existing conditions
* Cashless hospital list **in your city**
* Claim settlement rules + exclusions
* Top-up/super top-up logic
