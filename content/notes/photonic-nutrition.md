We treat light like a tool to see in the dark, but the body treats light as information. 

When blue light hits the retina at 10 PM, it sends a chemical signal to the brain that the sun is up. The brain instantly halts the production of melatonin (the sleep hormone). We are technologically advanced, but biologically ancient. To protect the family's circadian rhythm, we have to engineer the lighting of the home to mimic the sun - bright and cool in the morning, dim and amber at night.
