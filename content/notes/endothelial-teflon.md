Inside every blood vessel is a microscopic, slippery gel layer called the glycocalyx. Think of it as the non-stick Teflon coating on a frying pan. 

When this layer is healthy, blood cells slide right past. Plaque cannot stick to it. But chronic high blood sugar and oxidative stress act like molecular sandpaper. They strip the Teflon away, exposing the raw vessel wall underneath. This is where heart disease actually begins - not with cholesterol, but with the degradation of the non-stick coating.
