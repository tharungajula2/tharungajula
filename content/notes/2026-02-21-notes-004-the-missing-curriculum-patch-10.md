## Module A — Rationality & Judgment (Truth-seeking Under Pressure)

### The Glitch (Symptom)

You see a WhatsApp forward: *“Board paper leaked”* / *“JEE pattern changed”*.
Your brain hits panic mode. You switch strategy mid-week. You waste hours. You spiral.

### The Patch (Mechanism)

Your brain has two compute modes:

* **Fast mode** = quick + emotional + pattern-based.
* **Slow mode** = deliberate + logical + energy-expensive.

Fast mode is useful for crossing roads.
It is garbage for deciding what to study or what to believe.
That mismatch is the bug. ([Scientific American][1])

**Patch Protocols (run tomorrow):**

* **Decision Tree Debug**

  * Write the choice.
  * List 3 likely outcomes.
  * Assign rough probabilities.
  * Choose the branch with the best expected outcome (not the loudest emotion).
* **Base-Rate Reality Check**

  * When fear spikes, ask: *“What’s the normal probability of this?”*
  * Example: people fear rare dangers; reality is usually boring and statistical. ([OpenAI][2])
* **“Five Whys” Root-Cause**

  * “Why did I fail?” × 5.
  * Fix the system cause, not the surface event.

### The Source Code (Evidence)

* Daniel Kahneman — dual-system thinking (“fast” vs “slow”). ([Scientific American][1])
* National Safety Council (risk base-rates example). ([OpenAI][2])
* Florida Museum / ISAF (rare-risk perception example). ([Deep Paper][3])

## Module B — Meta-Learning (How Memory Actually Saves)

### The Glitch (Symptom)

You do a 6-hour cram with lo-fi beats.
You score okay.
Two days later the chapter feels wiped like RAM.

### The Patch (Mechanism)

Memory isn’t “stored.” It’s **reconstructed**.
To make it stable, you need **repeated retrieval over time**.

That’s the **spacing effect**: spaced practice beats cramming across hundreds of experiments. ([PubMed][4])

Sleep is not optional. Sleep is the **save operation**.
When sleep is cut, learning degrades — but spacing can protect recall even under sleep restriction. ([Injury Facts][5])

Teen hardware needs **8–10 hours**. That’s not “ideal.” That’s spec. ([Sleep Foundation][6])

**Patch Protocols (run tomorrow):**

* **Spaced Repetition Autopilot**

  * Review at: **+1 day, +3 days, +7 days, +14 days**
  * Use flashcards or question banks, but the key is **retrieval**, not rereading.
* **Cornell Notes (Anti-chaos formatting)**

  * Notes area + cue questions + 3-line summary.
  * Forces your brain to compress and index. ([Learning Strategies Center][7])
* **Handwriting > Typing (for understanding)**

  * Typing captures words.
  * Writing forces processing. ([Google Books][8])

### The Source Code (Evidence)

* Cepeda et al. (2006) meta-analysis: distributed practice works. ([PubMed][4])
* “Spaced learning protects memory against sleep restriction” (Sleep journal). ([Injury Facts][5])
* National Sleep Foundation: teen sleep duration. ([Sleep Foundation][6])
* Cornell Learning Strategies Center: Cornell notes. ([Learning Strategies Center][7])
* Mueller & Oppenheimer (2014): longhand vs laptop notes. ([Google Books][8])

## Module C — Applied Numeracy (Money, Risk, and “Not Getting Scammed”)

### The Glitch (Symptom)

You think:

* “I’ll start saving later.”
* “This investment doubles money fast.”
* “Percentages are school stuff.”

Then adulthood arrives with EMI, inflation, and regret.

### The Patch (Mechanism)

Most real-world math is **exponential**, not linear.

**Compounding** = interest on interest.
Small gains + time = big outcomes.
India literally has an investor education page screaming this in plain language. ([SEBI Investor][9])

Risk literacy = stop trusting vibes.
Compute basic probabilities.
Don’t let headlines set your fear settings. ([OpenAI][2])

**Patch Protocols (run tomorrow):**

* **Rule of 72**

  * Years to double ≈ `72 / rate%`. ([SEBI Investor][9])
* **Expected Value (EV)**

  * EV = probability × payoff − cost
  * If EV is negative, it’s not “luck.” It’s a designed loss.
* **Mini-Investing Sandbox**

  * Track a fake portfolio for 30 days.
  * Learn volatility without burning real money.

### The Source Code (Evidence)

* SEBI Investor: power of compounding + Rule of 72. ([SEBI Investor][9])
* Rule of 72 explanation (general finance reference). ([Investopedia][10])
* Risk base-rate references (risk perception calibration). ([OpenAI][2])
* S&P Global FinLit Survey (India literacy gap context). ([Deep Paper][3])

## Module D — Mental Hygiene (Dopamine, Attention, Emotional Cooling)

### The Glitch (Symptom)

* “Just one reel” becomes 2 AM.
* You can’t start studying without checking notifications.
* Mood swings: numb → anxious → guilty.

### The Patch (Mechanism)

Apps run a **reward schedule**: variable rewards + frequent triggers.
That combo can create behavioral addiction dynamics. ([ScienceDirect][11])

Adolescents are especially vulnerable because social reward circuitry is sensitive — likes aren’t “nothing.” They’re signals. ([PubMed][12])

Notifications aren’t harmless:

* Interruptions increase stress and task-switching cost. ([The Guardian][13])
* A 2026 study found a **~7-second processing slowdown** after social media notifications. ([ScienceDirect][14])

**Patch Protocols (run tomorrow):**

* **Notification Firewall**

  * Kill everything except calls from family + school essentials.
  * No banners on lock screen.
* **Focus Mode = Exam Mode**

  * 60–90 min blocks.
  * Phone in another room (distance beats willpower).
* **Breathing “CPU Cooldown”**

  * 4 slow breaths when panic spikes.
  * This isn’t spiritual. It’s physiology. Even a 1-minute guided breathing video reduced children’s stress arousal in field settings. ([Stanford Education][15])

### The Source Code (Evidence)

* Reward variability & behavioral addiction framing (Addictive Behaviors, 2023). ([ScienceDirect][11])
* “Power of the Like” (adolescent neural/behavioral responses). ([PubMed][12])
* “Cost of interrupted work” (CHI 2008). ([The Guardian][13])
* Notifications → ~7s processing slowdown (Computers in Human Behavior, 2026). ([ScienceDirect][14])
* Stanford study: brief slow breathing reduces physiological arousal in kids. ([Stanford Education][15])

## Module E — Media Literacy (Fake News Immunity + Privacy Basics)

### The Glitch (Symptom)

You forward a claim because:

* it matches your belief,
* it scares you,
* it came from “a smart person’s” story.

Later: it’s false. You lose credibility.

### The Patch (Mechanism)

The internet is not a library. It’s a battlefield.

The winning move is not “read carefully.”
The winning move is **lateral reading**: open new tabs, check the source, check other coverage, trace to originals. Fact-checkers do this. ([WFSU News][16])

Use **SIFT** as your default verification loop:

* **Stop**
* **Investigate**
* **Find better coverage**
* **Trace to the original** ([DPDPA.com][17])

Privacy: your data is currency.
India’s DPDP law treats kids’ data as sensitive: parental consent + no tracking/targeted ads aimed at children (with phased enforcement via Rules). ([India Code][18])

**Patch Protocols (run tomorrow):**

* **SIFT before you share**
* **Two-source rule**

  * One source is a claim.
  * Two independent sources is evidence.
* **Permissions diet**

  * If a “quiz app” wants contacts/location, uninstall it.

### The Source Code (Evidence)

* Stanford/SHEG: lateral reading + civic online reasoning. ([WFSU News][16])
* Mike Caulfield: SIFT method. ([DPDPA.com][17])
* Digital Personal Data Protection Act (official India Code PDF). ([India Code][18])
* DPDP Rules notification + timelines summary. ([PwC][19])

## Module F — AI Literacy (Using LLMs Without Getting Hallucinated)

### The Glitch (Symptom)

You ask a chatbot a question.
It answers confidently.
You paste it into homework.
Teacher catches bogus facts / fake citations.

### The Patch (Mechanism)

LLMs are **autocomplete on steroids**: pattern prediction, not truth engines.
They can produce **hallucinations** (confidently wrong outputs). OpenAI explicitly warns about this and tells users to verify important info. ([OUP Academic][20])

**Patch Protocols (run tomorrow):**

* **Use AI like a junior intern**

  * Good at drafting.
  * Bad at guarantees.
* **“Cite or delete” rule**

  * If it can’t point to a real source you can verify, treat it as untrusted.
* **Unit test the output**

  * For science/history: verify 3 key claims with reliable sources.
  * For math: recompute.
  * For code: run it.

### The Source Code (Evidence)

* OpenAI guidance: models can hallucinate; verify important outputs. ([OUP Academic][20])
* OpenAI (GPT-3) technical framing (autoregressive language model context). ([Legislative Dashboard][21])
