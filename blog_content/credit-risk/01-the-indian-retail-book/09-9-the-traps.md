# §9 · THE TRAPS

Nine ways to get caught, each drawn from something in this document.

1. **Quoting an aggregate.** "Retail grew 16.6%" is nearly content-free. Growth was 50.4% in gold and 0.0% in cards.
2. **Treating gold's growth as credit demand.** It is mostly collateral revaluation plus an LTV relaxation plus reclassification. §2.2.
3. **Reading falling gold delinquency as improving credit.** It is a bullion price effect. §2.2.
4. **Reporting improving unsecured delinquency from early buckets only.** PAR 180+ is 5.3% in personal loans and 6.9% in cards, and neither is moving. §2.3, §2.5.
5. **Analysing a shrinking revolving book with ratios alone.** Put the rupee number next to it. §2.5.
6. **Comparing PAR across products.** Two-wheelers at 4% and mortgages at 2% are not comparable businesses. §2.6.
7. **Reconciling GNPA to bureau PAR without mentioning write-offs.** §5.2.
8. **Reading a Q4 sequential fall in vehicles, cards or durables as deterioration.** Festive seasonality. §2.4.
9. **Confusing PAR, NPA, Ind AS staging and Basel EL.** §8.1.

And the meta-trap: **presenting a rule that has changed.** Two live examples in this document — the consumer credit risk weight was partially reversed in February 2025 but only for microfinance and NBFC exposure, and the credit information reporting move was deferred from 1 April to 1 July 2026. Getting either of those wrong signals that you learned the market from a course rather than from the circulars.
