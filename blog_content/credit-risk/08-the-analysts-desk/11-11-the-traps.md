# §11 · THE TRAPS

1. **Letting Run consume Answer.** Producing the pack on time is expected, not distinguishing. §1.1.
2. **Working from a current-state table** instead of monthly snapshots — which makes vintage, transition, backtesting and stability monitoring impossible. §2.1.
3. **Modelling with current attributes against historical outcomes.** Silent leakage, and it produces suspiciously good models. §2.2.
4. **Re-running instead of archiving.** Freeze every published pack. §2.2.
5. **Analytical DPD that does not match the regulatory convention** — day-end, borrower level, FIFO. §2.2.
6. **Publishing before reconciling to the general ledger.** One unreconciled number costs you every subsequent number. §2.3.
7. **Confusing a regulatory number with a management number** instead of maintaining the bridge. §4.2.
8. **Presenting analysis instead of a decision.** §5.1.
9. **Omitting the money sentence.** 1.9% to 2.4% sounds small; ₹190 crore does not. §5.2.
10. **Putting numbers in the pack that nobody would act on**, which trains the room to stop reading. §5.3.
11. **Starting an investigation with segment cuts** before ruling out artefacts and checking the numerator. §6, Steps 1–2.
12. **Not reading the shape.** MOB 2 is sourcing or fraud; MOB 6 is credit. §6, Step 5.
13. **Treating the aggregate as the finding.** The aggregate is the alarm. §6.
14. **A model document with no limitations section.** §7.1.
15. **Skipping implementation testing.** A correct model wrongly implemented is invisible in every performance statistic. §7.2.
16. **Hedging until the statement is useless.** Point estimate, range, driver, trigger. §8.2.
17. **Quoting Gini and PSI to a board.** Translate to consequence. §8.3.
18. **Building a model in month one.** §10.
