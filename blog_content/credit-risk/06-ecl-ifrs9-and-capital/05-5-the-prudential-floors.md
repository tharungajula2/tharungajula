# §5 · THE PRUDENTIAL FLOORS

## 5.1 What they are

📘 **A prudential floor is a minimum provision that applies regardless of what the bank's model produces.** Where IFRS 9 is principle-based, the RBI has added **product-wise minimum provisioning floors** as regulatory backstops.

`[VERIFY — figures from commentary, not paragraph-level reading]`

- **Stage 1**: reported at **0.40%** for standard corporate and retail exposures.
- **Stage 2**: reported at a minimum of **1%** of Stage 2 exposure.
- **Stage 3**: floors varying by **asset class and duration of default**, set so that ECL cannot produce a lower provision than the existing IRAC schedule.
- Applied **at product level**, across retail, corporate, MSME, agriculture and real estate.

Banks argued for lower floors during consultation. The RBI held.

## 5.2 Where the floor binds, worked

🧮 **Two segments, each with ₹10,000 crore of Stage 1 exposure.**

**Prime home loans.** Modelled 12-month PD 0.35%, LGD 20%.
Modelled ECL = 0.35% × 20% = **0.07%** → ₹7 crore.
Floor at 0.40% → **₹40 crore.**
**The floor binds, at roughly 5.7 times the model's output.**

**Unsecured personal loans.** Modelled 12-month PD 4.0%, LGD 85%.
Modelled ECL = 4.0% × 85% = **3.40%** → ₹340 crore.
Floor at 0.40% → ₹40 crore.
**The model binds, comfortably.**

🔴 **Three consequences, and the third is the one worth carrying.**

**One — in your best segments the model is decorative.** Document 05 §7.2 stated this; here is the arithmetic. No amount of modelling refinement on a prime mortgage book will change the reported Stage 1 provision, because the floor is 5.7× above where the model lands. Know where that boundary sits before allocating modelling effort.

**Two — the floor is a statement about trust, not about risk.** The regulator is not claiming prime mortgages lose 0.40%. It is capping the extent to which a bank can provision down on the strength of its own unvalidated models.

**Three — the floor compresses the measured risk differential between products at Stage 1**, which weakens ECL as a *pricing* signal in exactly the segments where competition is fiercest. If provisions on prime housing and on a moderately risky secured product are both at the floor, the provision no longer distinguishes them, and a bank pricing off reported provisions rather than off modelled expected loss will mis-price. **Use the model for pricing and the floor for reporting; do not let the second contaminate the first.**
