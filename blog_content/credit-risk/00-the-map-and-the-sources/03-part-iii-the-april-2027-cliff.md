# PART III — THE APRIL 2027 CLIFF

*Two Directions. Same issue date. Same commencement date. They interlock.*

## 3.1 What happened on 27 April 2026

RBI issued two instruments on the same day, both effective **1 April 2027**.

**Instrument one — the P&L side.** The **Reserve Bank of India (Commercial Banks – Asset Classification, Provisioning and Income Recognition) Directions, 2026.** <cite index="77-1">Its own short title clause names it precisely that, with commencement from 1 April 2027, and it repeals the 2025 IRACP Directions on commencement.</cite> <cite index="34-1">It followed the draft released for public consultation on 7 October 2025, and the final version incorporates select changes and clarifications on the application of prudential floors, ECL computation for purchased or originated credit-impaired assets, and determination of the effective interest rate.</cite>

**Instrument two — the balance sheet side.** The **Reserve Bank of India (Commercial Banks – Capital Charge for Credit Risk – Standardised Approach) Directions, 2026.** <cite index="45-1">Issued to implement the Basel III framework for calculating risk-weighted assets, applicable from 1 April 2027, covering sovereigns, banks, corporates, MSMEs, retail, real estate and off-balance-sheet items.</cite> <cite index="50-1">It followed the draft of 7 October 2025 and incorporates feedback published in an accompanying annex.</cite>

**The relationship, in one line:** <cite index="49-1">ECL regulations change how banks recognise credit risk on the P&L; the Credit Risk Capital Charge Directions change how they capitalise it on the balance sheet.</cite>

**They are not independent.** <cite index="49-1">The months leading to April 2027 absorb the Credit Risk SA recalibration, the ECL transition and SA-CCR refinements together, intertwined through NPA risk weights and credit-risk-mitigation logic.</cite> And as noted in §1.2, the ACPIR Directions borrow real-estate definitions directly from the capital Directions.

## 3.2 Applicability — and a definitional nuance worth getting right

Sources describe the perimeter two different ways and both are defensible, so state it carefully.

<cite index="76-1">The ACPIR Directions apply to 'Commercial Banks' — that is, banking companies other than Small Finance Banks, Payments Banks and Local Area Banks, plus corresponding new banks and the State Bank of India, as defined in the Banking Regulation Act, 1949.</cite>

<cite index="42-1">The Governor's October 2025 announcement described the perimeter as all Scheduled Commercial Banks excluding SFBs, Payments Banks and RRBs, plus All India Financial Institutions.</cite>

**The reconciliation:** the final Directions define the perimeter through the *definition of "Commercial Banks"* — which excludes SFBs, PBs and LABs — while RRBs sit outside that definition by their own constitution rather than by an express carve-out. AIFIs are addressed through a parallel route. [VERIFY — read the applicability clause of the notified Directions directly before stating the perimeter in a formal document; this is precisely the kind of detail where secondary summaries drift.]

**The safe formulation in an interview:** *"Commercial banks as defined in the Directions — so excluding small finance banks, payments banks and local area banks — plus AIFIs. Not RRBs. Not NBFCs, who are already on Ind AS 109."*

## 3.3 The transition timeline

| Date | What happens |
|:--|:--|
| 16 Jan 2023 | RBI Discussion Paper on Introduction of Expected Credit Loss Framework for Provisioning by Banks |
| 1 Oct 2025 | Governor announces ECL and revised Basel III for 1 April 2027, with glide path to 31 March 2031 |
| 7 Oct 2025 | Draft ACPIR Directions **and** draft Credit Risk SA Directions issued for consultation |
| **27 Apr 2026** | **Both sets of final Directions notified**, with a Statement on Feedback Received |
| 31 Mar 2027 | Closing balance sheet under the 2025 IRACP Directions |
| **1 Apr 2027** | **Both Directions commence.** 2025 IRACP Directions repealed. Opening ECL computed on the 31 March 2027 balance sheet |
| 31 Mar 2030 | <cite index="76-1">Deadline for full migration of all outstanding loans to the EIR regime</cite> |
| 31 Mar 2031 | <cite index="42-1">End of the glide path for spreading the one-time provisioning impact</cite> |

**Two details in that table that people miss.**

<cite index="38-1">For loans originated on or after 1 April 2027, a bank measures the financial asset at fair value plus or minus directly attributable transaction costs, and thereafter at amortised cost using the EIR method. All loans outstanding as on 31 March 2027 must be brought under the EIR regime no later than 31 March 2030.</cite> <cite index="76-1">For opening ECL as on 1 April 2027, banks may as a transitional measure use the contractual interest rate as the discount factor.</cite>

**So there are two glide paths, not one**, and they end in different years. The EIR migration runs to March 2030; the capital/P&L impact smoothing runs to March 2031. Confusing them is a real error.

## 3.4 Why India chose Standardised Approach only

This is the strategic fact that should reorganise how you study capital, and it is the biggest single correction to most existing material.

<cite index="49-1">BCBS permits both SA and IRB. RBI has chosen to implement only the Standardised Approach — signalling comparability over complexity, and avoiding the model-driven RWA compression that has drawn criticism of IRB globally and necessitated the EU output floor in CRR3.</cite>

<cite index="43-1">The guidelines show reduced emphasis on internal models, with greater reliance on a prescriptive approach including external ratings.</cite>

**What this means for your study time.** The Basel IRB risk-weight function — the Vasicek correlation formula, the stressed-PD conditional expectation, the maturity adjustment — is **background knowledge for an Indian bank role, not operating knowledge.** It's worth being able to explain the intuition, because interviewers ask and because it explains *why* the SA numbers are calibrated where they are. It is not worth the twenty pages your old textbook gave it.

**What replaces it** — and this is genuinely where the work is now — is the SA taxonomy: the regulatory retail portfolio criteria, the granularity test, the product carve-outs, transactor versus revolver, the LTV-banded real-estate treatment, CCF including the new treatment of unconditionally cancellable commitments, and the ODR-based overlay on rating agencies. That's Document 04.

**And India is not late.** <cite index="49-1">The EU's CRR3 took effect 1 January 2025, the UK's Basel 3.1 is scheduled for 1 January 2027, and the US Basel III endgame remains in finalisation. India's 1 April 2027 falls squarely within the same global cohort — arriving in sequence, not catching up.</cite> That framing is worth having, because you will be asked how India compares.
