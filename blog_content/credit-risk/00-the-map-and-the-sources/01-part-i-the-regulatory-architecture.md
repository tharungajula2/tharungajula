# PART I — THE REGULATORY ARCHITECTURE

## 1.1 Who regulates what

For retail credit risk in India, the answer is short: **RBI regulates essentially everything you touch.**

**RBI** — commercial banks, small finance banks, payments banks, local area banks, regional rural banks, co-operative banks, NBFCs (including HFCs since 2019), All India Financial Institutions, ARCs, credit information companies under CICRA 2005, and the payment systems.

**NHB** retains a supervisory role in housing finance but **regulatory authority over HFCs moved to RBI in 2019.** Getting this wrong is a live tell — a lot of older material still says NHB regulates HFCs.

**SEBI and IRDAI** sit outside retail credit risk. **MCA** matters for one reason only: it notifies the Indian Accounting Standards, including **Ind AS 109**, which is the accounting spine of everything in Document 03.

**The one distinction to hold:** RBI issues *prudential* norms; MCA notifies *accounting* standards. For NBFCs these two run in parallel and sometimes conflict, and the conflict is resolved by a specific RBI instruction covered in Document 03. For banks, prudential norms have historically been the only game — and that is exactly what changes in April 2027.

## 1.2 The October 2025 consolidation — the structural fact nobody's textbook has

This is the most important thing in Part I and it is why most secondary material you find will cite dead instruments.

<cite index="81-1">In October 2025 the RBI consolidated its existing regulatory instructions into 238 Master Directions covering 11 types of regulated entities across 30 areas, repealing around 9,000 circulars including previous Master Circulars and Directions.</cite> [VERIFY — the exact counts and the cut-off date for instructions covered; confirm against the RBI press release before quoting figures.]

**What this means practically, and it is not cosmetic:**

The familiar "**Master Circular – Prudential Norms on Income Recognition, Asset Classification and Provisioning pertaining to Advances**" — the document every credit risk course in India taught for twenty years — **is no longer the operative instrument.** Today's rule is the **Reserve Bank of India (Commercial Banks – Income Recognition, Asset Classification and Provisioning) Directions, 2025**. <cite index="77-1">The 2026 ACPIR Directions state explicitly that banks continue to be governed by the 2025 IRACP Directions until commencement, on which the 2025 Directions stand repealed.</cite>

**Three consequences for you:**

**Any material citing a Master Circular by number is presumptively stale.** That includes most blog posts, most training decks, and your old textbook.

**Instrument naming now follows a pattern**, and learning it saves you real time: *Reserve Bank of India (Entity Class – Subject Matter) Directions, Year*. Once you see the pattern, you can guess the name of an instrument you haven't read and search for it accurately.

**Definitions are now cross-referenced between Directions.** <cite index="77-1">The ACPIR Directions define CRE(ADC) and CRE-RH(ADC) by pointing at the Capital Charge for Credit Risk Directions, 2026.</cite> You cannot read one instrument in isolation any more, which is a real change in how the rulebook works.

## 1.3 The instrument hierarchy

Read downward. Each level binds the ones below it.

**Statute** — Banking Regulation Act 1949; RBI Act 1934; Credit Information Companies (Regulation) Act 2005; Companies Act 2013 (for Ind AS); SARFAESI 2002; IBC 2016. RBI's Directions are issued under specific sections — the ACPIR and capital Directions cite **Sections 21 and 35A of the BR Act**.

**Master Directions / Directions** — the consolidated, binding rulebook. This is what you cite.

**Circulars and notifications** — issued between consolidations, amending or clarifying Directions. These accumulate until the next consolidation folds them in.

**FAQs** — RBI publishes these for several frameworks. **Not legally binding but supervisorily meaningful**, and they answer the questions your implementation will actually hit.

**Discussion papers and drafts** — signal direction of travel. The January 2023 ECL Discussion Paper is the ancestor of the 2026 Directions.

**A practitioner habit worth forming now:** when you cite something in a meeting, cite the *Directions* and the *paragraph*, not the circular you read about it in. "Para 14(3) of the Credit Risk SA Directions" lands very differently from "I read that RBI changed the retail threshold."
