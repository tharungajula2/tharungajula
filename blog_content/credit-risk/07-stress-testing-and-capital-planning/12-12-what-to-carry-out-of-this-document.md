# §12 · WHAT TO CARRY OUT OF THIS DOCUMENT

1. **ECL weights the downside; a stress test assumes it.** The gap between ₹121 crore and ₹180 crore is what capital is for.
2. **India's stack: CET1 5.5%, Tier 1 7%, CRAR 9%, plus a 2.5% CCB, plus a D-SIB surcharge of 0.20% to 0.80%.** One point above Basel throughout.
3. **A buffer is meant to be used.** Breaching it restricts distributions; it is not a breach of the minimum.
4. **The CCyB has never been activated in India** — the trigger is the credit-to-GDP gap, and India prefers targeted sectoral tools instead.
5. **The FSR's 8.1% is a sensitivity, not a scenario**, and the NBFC results are the more consequential half.
6. **The weakest link in any Indian retail stress test is the macro-to-default satellite model**, because the only recent downturn was contaminated by moratoria.
7. **Report the P&L, not just the ratio.** A bank can be capital-adequate through a stress and still lose a year's earnings.
8. **Reverse stress testing is the only exercise you cannot calibrate your way out of.**
9. **Pillar 1 is blind to concentration.** That is Pillar 2's largest single job.
10. **RAROC, not margin.** The 30%-weighted mortgage beats the 125%-weighted personal loan at a third of the spread — which explains Document 01's whole growth pattern.
11. **From April 2027, stress arrives through Stage 2 migration, before any account misses a payment.**
