# §8 · DOCUMENT 07 — STRESS TESTING AND CAPITAL PLANNING

**ECL versus a stress test, in one line each.**
ECL multiplies the downside by its probability and asks what we expect to lose. A stress test asks what happens if the downside simply occurs.

**Where does capital fit in that?**
In the gap. Probability-weighted ECL was ₹121 crore; the downside alone was ₹180 crore. The ₹59 crore difference is what capital is for.

**Recite India's capital stack.**
CET1 5.5%, Tier 1 7.0%, total CRAR 9.0% — a full point above Basel throughout — plus a 2.5% capital conservation buffer, a countercyclical buffer of 0 to 2.5% currently at zero, and a D-SIB surcharge of 0.20% to 1.00%.

**Effective CET1 requirement for SBI?**
5.5 + 2.5 + 0.80 = **8.30%**. HDFC Bank 8.10%, ICICI 7.95%.

**System CRAR is 17.7%. So?**
Roughly 5.8 percentage points of headroom over the most demanding requirement — which is why the FSR's severe scenario puts only four banks, 12% of assets, below minimum.

**Is a buffer a minimum?**
No. It's capital you're expected to **use** in bad times. Breaching it restricts discretionary distributions — dividends, buybacks, bonuses — on a sliding scale; it isn't a breach of the requirement.

**And the practical problem with that design?**
Banks are reluctant to use buffers because a visible drawdown invites market punishment, so buffers behave more like minimums than intended.

**Has India ever activated the CCyB?**
No — never since the February 2015 framework, most recently declining on 18 May 2026. The main indicator is the credit-to-GDP gap.

**Gold grew 50%. Why no CCyB?**
Because the trigger is system-wide credit relative to GDP, not any one segment. India addresses sectoral froth with sectoral tools — risk weights, LTV caps, digital lending rules.

**Give the FSR June 2026 stress results.**
GNPA 1.8% at March 2026, baseline 1.9% by March 2028, adverse scenarios 3.8% to 4.1%. Severe credit shock at 8.1% GNPA puts four banks, 12% of assets, below minimum CRAR.

**What's the trap in quoting that 8.1%?**
It's a single-factor **sensitivity**, not a scenario. It isn't comparable to the 4.1%.

**Which half of the FSR results matters more, and why?**
The NBFC half. Eleven NBFCs below the 15% CRAR requirement in the worst case matters more for household credit availability than four banks, because NBFCs write the overwhelming majority of unsecured retail volume.

**Recite the stress transmission chain.**
Macro scenario → risk parameters → asset quality → provisions → P&L → capital → ratios.

**Which link is weakest and why?**
Macro to parameters — the satellite models — because the Indian retail default series is contaminated by moratoria. Everything downstream inherits that weakness.

**A stress halves capital headroom but the ratio holds. What do you report?**
Both — and lead with the P&L. A bank can be entirely capital-adequate through a stress and still lose a year's earnings, and it's the loss that determines whether it can raise capital or hold its cost of funds.

**What is reverse stress testing and why is it the most valuable exercise?**
It starts from non-viability and works backwards to find the scenarios that produce it. It's valuable because conventional stress testing lets the institution choose the severity, and nobody proposes a scenario that breaks their own bank. Fixing the outcome removes that degree of freedom.

**What are its three outputs?**
The binding constraint — what breaks first. The early indicators — what would move first if it were beginning. And pre-agreed, sequenced contingency actions, realistic about availability in a stressed market.

**What's the largest gap in Pillar 1 that ICAAP must fill?**
Concentration risk. Under the standardised approach, a hundred loans to one sector and a hundred diversified loans carry identical RWA.

**How do you judge an ICAAP?**
By the use test. Not "is it well written" but "show me a business decision that changed because of it."

**Compare a 30%-weighted home loan and a 125%-weighted personal loan on RAROC.**
Home loan: net margin 1.10% over capital of 3.57% — **RAROC 30.8%**. Personal loan: net margin 3.60% over capital of 14.88% — **RAROC 24.2%**. The mortgage wins at a third of the spread, because it consumes a quarter of the capital.

**And what does that explain?**
Document 01's entire growth pattern. The book grew where capital was cheap because RAROC said so.

**One subtlety in that comparison.**
The home loan's expected loss was taken at the 0.40% floor. Use the modelled 0.07% and RAROC rises to 40.1% — so allocating off floored provisions understates true return by about a third.

**From April 2027, how does stress reach capital differently?**
Through Stage 2 migration rather than NPA formation. SICR triggers at 30 DPD, and also on a lifetime-PD increase that can hit fully current accounts, and each migration multiplies the allowance. **Provisions rise before any account misses a payment.**

**Name three things that damp the procyclicality.**
The four-year transition, for day one only. The prudential floors, which are counter-cyclical by construction because they hold provisions above the model in good times. And SA-only capital, which stops RWA inflating as PDs rise — India removed one of the two procyclical channels.
