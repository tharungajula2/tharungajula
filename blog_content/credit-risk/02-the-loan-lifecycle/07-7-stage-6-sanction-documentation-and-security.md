# §7 · STAGE 6 — SANCTION, DOCUMENTATION AND SECURITY

## 7.1 The decision

Is the lender's claim legally enforceable, and is the security perfected? This stage produces no interesting data and almost no analytics, and it is where a surprising share of realised loss originates — because **an unenforceable claim has an LGD of 100% regardless of how good the collateral is.**

## 7.2 The three forms of security in retail

| Form | Used for | Mechanism |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **Mortgage** | Home loans, loan against property | Charge over immovable property; equitable mortgage by deposit of title deeds is the common Indian form |
| **Hypothecation** | Vehicles, consumer durables | Charge over movable property that stays in the borrower's possession |
| **Pledge** | Gold loans | Movable property delivered into the lender's possession |

📘 **The distinction that matters for LGD.** Under a **pledge** the lender already holds the asset — realisation is fast and certain, which is why gold-loan LGD is low and why Document 01 §2.2 showed gold delinquency collapsing during a price rally. Under **hypothecation** the lender must take possession from a borrower who has it — hence repossession machinery, and the observable but costly recovery that gives auto loans their improving PAR 180+. Under a **mortgage** of immovable property the lender must enforce through a legal process. Same word "secured", three completely different loss profiles.

## 7.3 Perfection and registration

Security interests over property are registered with **CERSAI** — the central registry — which serves the essential function of preventing the same property being pledged twice to different lenders. An unregistered charge is a charge you may not be able to enforce against a competing claimant.

## 7.4 The repayment mandate

**NACH** — the National Automated Clearing House mandate — or an e-mandate, authorising the lender to debit the borrower's account on the due date.

This is a control point disguised as an operational detail. **The bounce is the earliest signal in the entire lifecycle.** A NACH presentation that fails on the due date is information the lender has *the same day*, weeks before the account becomes SMA-1 and months before it becomes an NPA. Bounce rate — and specifically first-presentation bounce rate on a recent origination cohort — is the highest-frequency early warning series a retail lender owns.

## 7.5 Release of documents on closure 

Now inside the RBC Directions: on full repayment or settlement, the lender must **release all original movable and immovable property documents and remove registered charges within a prescribed period**, with compensation payable for delay. The gold-lending directions carry an equivalent obligation to return pledged gold within a short window.

This exists because it was routinely not done, and the delay is now expensive.

**► SAY THIS**
> "Documentation is where I'd expect an under-examined source of loss. 'Secured' isn't one thing — a pledge means the lender already holds the asset, hypothecation means they have to take it from someone who has it, and a mortgage means they have to enforce through a process. Same label, three very different LGDs. And the operational control I'd care most about here is actually the repayment mandate, because a first-presentation NACH bounce is same-day information about an account that won't be an NPA for another ninety days."
