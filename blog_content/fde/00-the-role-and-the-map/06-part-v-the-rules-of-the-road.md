## PART V — THE RULES OF THE ROAD

Five conventions run through every document. Learn them here and they will never need re-explaining.

**OUTPUT blocks.** Every piece of code is followed by exactly what the terminal would print. You never have to imagine, guess, or run anything.

**MENTAL TRACE.** After the output, a plain-English narration of what happened line by line — what each variable became, why the output looks the way it does. Code, then result, then the story of the result. Reading the three in sequence is functionally equivalent to having executed it. This is what makes the set fully portable: a phone on a bus is a complete learning environment.

**THE DEPLOYMENT LENS.** Appearing where it genuinely earns its place — not on every section: how this concept breaks inside a real customer's environment, which of the five people above will push back on it, and the sentence you actually say out loud in the room. This is the difference between knowing a thing and being deployable with it.

**`[VERIFY]`.** Attached to any fact that rots — model names, prices, regulation numbers, library versions, effective dates. It marks the sentence as needing a live check before you stake anything on it. Anything untagged is either stable or verified. The SR 11-7 correction above is precisely why this convention is not optional.

**`[RECEIPT]`.** Sparse and deliberate. Marks the roughly fifteen things across the whole set that, once actually built, become portfolio evidence. These are your answer to the experience gate. Reading gets you understanding; receipts get you interviews.

**No summaries, no recap cards, no repetition.** If a section is written well, restating it insults it. All compression lives in Document 09, once, at the end.
