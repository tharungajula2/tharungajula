## § 8.5 — The demo

A demo is not a status update. **A demo is an argument**, and most engineers make the wrong one.

The wrong argument is *"look what it can do."* The right argument is *"look what it does to your work, and here's how you'd know if I'm lying."*

### The craft

**Let them pick the input.** The single highest-trust move available. A demo on a file you chose proves nothing and everyone in the room knows it. Handing the file selection to the skeptic — Dan, Tom — changes the entire epistemic status of what follows.

**Show the failure on purpose.** Document 05's demo showed a regressed golden case and it was the moment the meeting turned. Every vendor before you demonstrated capability. **Demonstrating that you measure your own failures and will show them is a different category of claim**, and it's the one a validation function is starving for.

**Never demo through a projector on a laptop that needs the customer's wifi.** Use demo mode from § 7.12. A demo that breaks because an API rate-limited you doesn't read as bad luck; it reads as an unreliable system.

**Make the domain expert the operator.** If Tom drives the demo, the room is watching an underwriter use a tool. If you drive it, they're watching a vendor perform. The second is worth a fraction of the first.

**End on the constraint, not the capability.** *"This is what it does. Here's what it can't do, and here's what I need from you to keep it moving."* A demo that ends on a request is a demo that produces a decision.

**THE DEPLOYMENT LENS.** There's a demo you'll be asked for that's genuinely dangerous: the executive demo, early, to someone who wasn't in discovery.

They will see a prototype and hear a product. They will repeat what they saw to their peers, with the caveats stripped, and eight weeks later you'll be held to a promise you didn't make.

**The defence is a sentence at the start, not a caveat at the end**: *"This is a nine-day prototype on twenty files. It's here to show the shape of the thing, not the quality of it. The quality numbers come in six weeks and I'll bring those separately."* Say it before you open the laptop, and say it in the follow-up email, because the email is what gets forwarded.
