## § 8.1 — What the week actually looks like

The role description is compact and the current OpenAI posting states it plainly: <cite index="25-1">you own discovery, technical scoping, system design, build, and production rollout, partnering directly with customer engineering and domain teams — and you measure success through production adoption, measurable workflow impact, and eval-driven feedback that changes product and model roadmaps.</cite>

Read the second half of that sentence again, because it's the part people skip. **Success is not "the system works."** Success is production adoption, measured workflow impact, and feedback that changes what your company builds.

The week, concretely, looks something like this: <cite index="29-1">Monday, a discovery workshop with a customer's data team, scoping which of their fragmented data sources matter. Tuesday, a coding marathon — Python for an integration, SQL for a reporting flow. Wednesday, building a thin frontend for the customer's operators and standing up service logic that works inside their auth and network constraints. Thursday, live incident response with the customer team waiting on a fix. Friday, explaining a technical compromise to a non-technical VP in business language, without watering down the truth.</cite>

Five days, five different disciplines. <cite index="29-1">The work is messy, but it's close to revenue, adoption, and product truth — you don't have to guess whether your work mattered, because the customer environment tells you immediately.</cite>

And the skill profile that follows: <cite index="29-1">T-shaped — deep expertise in one core area, broad capability across several others, and a strong bar of customer-facing soft skills.</cite>

**Two honest notes about the shape of the job**, because they matter to a life and not just a career.

**Travel is real.** The current OpenAI listing specifies <cite index="25-1">travel up to 50%</cite>. Some engagements are remote, many are not, and being physically present is often *why* the role works — you find things sitting next to someone that you never find on a call.

**And Friday's task is the hardest one on that list.** Explaining a compromise to a non-technical VP without watering down the truth is a genuine skill, most engineers are bad at it, and § 8.6 is entirely about it.
