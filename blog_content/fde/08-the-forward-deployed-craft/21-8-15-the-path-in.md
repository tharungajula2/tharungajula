## § 8.15 — The path in

The honest section, because the stakes here are real.

### The market

<cite index="31-1">Hiring interest in Forward Deployed Engineers grew 800% between January 2025 and mid-2026, a16z called it the hottest job in tech, OpenAI grew its FDE practice from two engineers to thirty-nine in a single year, and Anthropic, Scale AI, Databricks and Salesforce all formalised versions of the model.</cite> <cite index="31-1">In March 2026 Accenture launched a dedicated forward deployed engineering practice.</cite>

Growing demand is real. **So is the bar**, and Document 00 stated it: most postings ask for 5+ years of engineering or technical deployment experience, often customer-facing.

### The three routes, concretely

**Route 1 — Evidence over credentials.** The screen is for demonstrated deployment competence. **Eight receipts in one coherent system, with an honest limitations document, is a stronger signal than a job title.** Write about it — not a tutorial, a *deployment story*: what you found in discovery, what you scoped out, what broke, what you measured, what you'd do differently. That artifact is unusual enough to open doors on its own.

**Route 2 — The adjacent door, and this is the realistic one.** Solutions Engineer. Implementation Engineer. AI Engineer at a company that sells to enterprises. Integration Engineer. Technical Consultant at a firm building a practice — <cite index="31-1">the consultancies are formalising these practices right now</cite>, and they hire earlier-career people that the frontier labs won't.

**The title is downstream of doing the work for eighteen months somewhere less famous.** That is not a consolation prize; it is the actual path, and it is faster than waiting to qualify for the front door.

**Route 3 — Domain leverage.** You now have credit-risk vocabulary, model-risk governance, adverse action, and fair-lending methodology. **Almost no engineer applying for these roles has any of it**, and regulated industries hire the most FDEs. Aim at lending, insurance, and financial-services AI companies specifically, and say the domain words in the first conversation.

### What the interviews test

Based on how these loops are structured: **AI system design** — architect a deployment and defend it. **Case and decomposition rounds** — turn a vague ask into scoped options with measurable success criteria. **AI-assisted pair coding.** And **behavioural and procurement-security rounds**, which is where Documents 06 and 07 pay.

The signature question is architecture under hostile questioning, and this set has been training you for it the whole way. When someone asks *"why not just fine-tune?"* or *"how do you secure an agent?"* or *"how do you know it's working?"*, you have real answers with real reasoning, and — more importantly — you have the instinct to answer with a *tradeoff* rather than a technique.

### The honest part

This is not fast. Route 2 is eighteen months to two years to the title, plus however long it takes to get in the adjacent door. **The compensation figures in Document 00 are for the destination, not the entrance**, and treating the entrance salary as failure is how people quit six months from the thing working.

And two things nobody mentions. **Travel up to 50%** is a real constraint on a life. **And the role is emotionally demanding** in a way pure engineering isn't — you are the face of your company in rooms where people are skeptical, sometimes threatened, and occasionally hostile, and you absorb that while still being the person who has to ship.

Both are survivable. Neither should be a surprise.

**What is genuinely true:** the scarcity is real, the skills compound, the domain edge you now have is unusual, and the work itself — <cite index="29-1">close to revenue, adoption, and product truth, where you don't have to guess whether your work mattered</cite> — is the kind most engineers say they want and few get.
