## § 8.12 — When it goes wrong

Because it will, and how you behave in the bad weeks is most of your reputation.

**Bad news early, in person, with a proposal.** *"The Spanish extraction problem is worse than I thought. It affects 20% of volume and I don't have a fix this quarter. Here are three options and what I'd pick."* Never let a stakeholder discover a problem from a dashboard.

**Own the failure, not the blame.** *"I should have tested on their real scan quality in week two"* is worth more than any explanation of why you didn't. And **never blame the customer's data, systems, or people in front of them**, even when it's their fault — especially then. Describe the constraint, not the culpability.

**Distinguish a slip from a wall.** A slip is "this takes three more weeks." A wall is "this approach doesn't work." **Engineers systematically report walls as slips**, for months, and it is the single most damaging habit in this role. If you're at a wall, say the word.

**And know when to recommend stopping.** Rare, and it is the highest-integrity act available to you. A project that should be cancelled and isn't consumes a year of your life and everyone's budget, and the person who says so first is remembered well — because it's evidence that your recommendations mean something.
